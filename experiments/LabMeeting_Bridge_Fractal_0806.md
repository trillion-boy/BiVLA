*(Korean edition: `LabMeeting_Bridge_Fractal_0806_KR.md` — both are written from
the same generated numbers.)*

**Status: campaign in progress.** Cells marked `--` have not been run. Every
number below is generated by `python experiments/build_grid_report.py` from the
per-episode records in `results/`; nothing is transcribed. Re-run it after each

---

## What we set out to do, and what happened

We set out to show that a cheap inference-time intervention — hold each action
for k environment steps, foveate the observation, skip redundant decoder layers
— buys wall-clock without costing success. That is a method claim, and it needs
the effect to be a property of the *method*.

It is not. Running the same code through the same hook points across four
backbones and two benchmarks, **the sign of the effect changes**. Not the
magnitude — the sign. So the finding is not "our method works" and it is not
"our method fails"; it is that a single-backbone, single-benchmark measurement
of an efficiency intervention does not predict what that intervention does
anywhere else. That is a claim about how this class of work is evaluated, and
the grid below is the evidence for it.

## Three things this supports

### 1. The same intervention reverses sign across backbones

Action repeat 2, identical code at an identical hook point:

| backbone | Δ | discordant | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| UniVLA | **−70.8** | 1 fixed / 69 broke | < 1e-15 |
| OpenVLA | −8.3 | 3 / 11 | 0.057 |
| SpatialVLA | **+12.5** | 21 / 9 | 0.043 |

An 83-point spread, with the two extremes both statistically resolved. This is
not a difference of degree that a better default would smooth out.

### 2. It also reverses sign across benchmarks, with the policy unchanged

| action repeat 2 | Bridge | Fractal |
|---|---|---|
| OpenVLA | −8.3 | **+9.3** (coke can only, p = 0.19) |
| SpatialVLA | **+12.5** (p = 0.043) | **+0.0** (p = 1.00) |

Same checkpoint, same weights, same intervention — only the benchmark moves.
**Neither backbone's Bridge result predicts its own Fractal result.** OpenVLA
flips sign; SpatialVLA's gain disappears.

*Caveat:* OpenVLA/Fractal repeat 2 currently covers the three coke-can tasks
(75 of 135 protocol episodes); `move_near_v0` is still running and can move the
number. Do not quote +9.3 as the task average yet.

### 3. Within one backbone and one benchmark, temporal ≫ visual sensitivity

SpatialVLA on Fractal, same session, same protocol, full 135 episodes:

| intervention | what it removes | Δ | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| action repeat 4 | re-planning at 3 of every 4 steps | **−40.0** | ≈ 0 |
| foveation log-polar 20% | 80% of visual sample density | +0.7 | 1.00 |
| foveation blur 20% | 80% of visual detail (geometry intact) | +1.3 ² | 1.00 |

Deleting four fifths of the visual information is invisible. Holding an action
for four steps destroys the policy. This is the only claim here that needs no
cross-benchmark caveat — it is one policy, one benchmark, one session.

² blur covers 75 of 135 episodes; `move_near_v0` still running.

Detection floor: with 15 discordant pairs, effects larger than ≈ ±7 points are
excluded. "No effect" here means "no effect of that size", not zero.

## The obvious explanation, and why it does not survive

The natural mechanism after the Bridge results was **distance from the trained
execution length**: OpenVLA (k=1) and UniVLA (k=5) are already deployed at their
trained length and can only lose by moving; SpatialVLA is the one backbone
deployed *below* its own action-chunk size, and it is the one that gains at
repeat 2. That story fits Bridge exactly.

It fails on Fractal. Same checkpoint, same chunk size, and repeat 2 goes from
+12.5 to +0.0 while repeat 4 goes from −12.5 to −40.0 — the peak at k=2 that
Bridge shows is simply absent. Whatever produces the gain is not the trained
chunk length alone, because that quantity did not change.

We should present this rather than hide it. "The most plausible mechanism does
not cross benchmarks either" is a stronger version of the same thesis than a
tidy explanation would be.

## One signal worth chasing

On SpatialVLA / Fractal `pick_standing_coke_can`, both foveation variants
rescue **exactly the same four baseline failures** (episodes 10, 17, 20, 23)
while breaking different ones:

| | failing episodes |
|---|---|
| baseline | 10, 17, 20, 23 |
| log-polar | 22 |
| blur | 16 |

Two unrelated image degradations — one moves pixels, one only removes detail —
fixing the same four episodes is not what noise looks like. It suggests those
episodes fail from *too much* visual information rather than too little. n = 25
and it is one task of four, so this is an observation, not a result; the next
step is to look at what is actually in those four scenes.

### Two liabilities

**Legacy cells.** Four foveation/depth cells in the older figure (SpatialVLA
−7.3 / −2.1, UniVLA +8.3, RoboVLMs −19.8 / −16.7) come from a campaign that kept
no per-episode records. They are unpaired at n = 96, where nothing below ~15
points is resolvable. They cannot carry a sign-reversal claim and should be
re-measured or dropped.

**Floor and ceiling.** SpatialVLA sits at 84% on Fractal, OpenVLA at 38%.
A +9 gain near the floor and a +9 gain near the ceiling are not the same
achievement. Report the baseline next to every delta rather than letting a
reader compare deltas across columns unguarded.
