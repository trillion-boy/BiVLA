\section{Related Work}

We cover three strands, namely the cost of VLA inference, the training-free
interventions we re-measure, and how such interventions are evaluated.

\textbf{Inference cost in VLA policies.} VLA policies adapt pretrained
vision-language models to output robot actions, inheriting their size and
latency, and the resulting efficiency literature already has surveys of its
own~\cite{effvlasurvey1, effvlasurvey2}. We divide it by the resource each
method spends, which recovers a recent survey's categories of dynamic
computation, perception and action generation~\cite{effvlasurvey1}.
1)~\emph{When the policy runs}. A predicted chunk is executed over several
control steps, a lineage running from frame skip in RL to parallel
decoding~\cite{dqn, act, diffusionpolicy, openvlaoft}. 2)~\emph{What it is
shown}. Visual tokens are reduced or reweighted. 3)~\emph{How much network
each call uses}. Decoder layers are skipped. We treat these as axes rather
than competing methods, because a claim about efficiency is a claim about one
of these resources being spent differently.

\textbf{The interventions we re-measure.} Layer redundancy in language models
is well established, but its two recipes disagree on which layers may go.
ShortGPT ranks every layer by Block Influence, the criterion we adopt, and
constrains nothing further~\cite{shortgpt}, while Gromov et al.\ remove a
contiguous block of the deepest layers and find the final layer must be
kept~\cite{gromov}. EfficientVLA carries the unconstrained form into VLAs
without training~\cite{efficientvla}, and MoLe-VLA needs a learned router and
distillation~\cite{molevla}, placing it outside what we test. The two rules
have not been compared on a robot policy, and our depth results turn on which
one holds. Notably, the newest compact VLAs build such reductions in by design
rather than applying them at inference~\cite{flower, smolvla, turbovla}, which
makes whether a reduction transfers a question about architecture and not only
about a switch. On the visual axis, methods developed for VLMs~\cite{fastv,
sparsevlm, tome} have been reported to transfer poorly to VLAs. VLA-Cache
attributes this to their short action sequences and measures FastV leaving
FLOPs unchanged while latency rises~\cite{vlacache}, and VLA-Pruner reproduces
the degradation on a third setting~\cite{vlapruner}. Our own visual
intervention, foveation, comes from the other direction. It descends from
Schwartz's log-polar mapping~\cite{schwartz}, taken up in robot vision to cut
data while preserving central resolution~\cite{traver} and applied to VLAs by
gaze-conditioned policies~\cite{gazereg, lookfocusact}. We include it because
it edits the observation \emph{before} the encoder, which is what makes it
comparable across backbones. No matter how the encoders differ, each splits
the image into a \emph{uniform} grid, so empty background gets the same budget
as the region where the gripper meets the object.

\textbf{How these claims are evaluated.} Results are reported on
SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} and LIBERO~\cite{libero}, and recent work
addresses the infrastructure around them. The vla-eval harness unifies
fourteen benchmarks and documents previously undocumented evaluation
pitfalls~\cite{vlaeval}, and StarVLA describes the field as fragmented across
incompatible codebases and protocols~\cite{starvla}. What infrastructure
cannot supply is the comparison itself. Papers using several backbones change
the benchmark at the same time~\cite{vlacache, specprune, vlaiap}, those with
both axes present leave the crossing cell empty~\cite{gazereg, vlapruner}, and
the tables we cite report mean success rates over independent runs, without
matched-episode outcomes. Whether an intervention keeps its \emph{direction}
when the backbone or the benchmark changes therefore cannot be read off these
tables, nor can the per-task disagreements they average over
(Table~\ref{tab:reporting}).

We do not cover quantisation, KV-cache compression or learned early exit,
which spend resources our axes do not. Measuring the settings papers leave to
their source code is an established form~\cite{bagoftricks_cnn,
bagoftricks_llm}. Unique to this paper, we measure the same interventions over
a complete backbone $\times$ benchmark grid and test each on matched episodes
rather than aggregate rates, so that the direction of an effect, and not only
its size, is something the evidence can decide.
