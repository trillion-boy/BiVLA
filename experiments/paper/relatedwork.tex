\section{Related Work}

Training-free VLA efficiency methods reduce cost along three parts of the inference loop: the visual information given to the policy, the network computation used in each policy call, and how often the policy is invoked. We review prior work along these axes and position the five interventions evaluated in this study.

\noindent\textbf{Visual Efficiency.}
A direct way to reduce visual cost is to avoid processing every image region with the same fidelity. Classical foveated vision preserves high resolution near a region of interest while compressing peripheral regions~\cite{schwartz,traver}, and recent robot-learning methods adopt related ideas through gaze-centered tokenization or gaze supervision to guide visual processing~\cite{lookfocusact,gazereg}. However, for transformer-based VLAs with a fixed visual-token grid, changing the image only in pixel space does not necessarily reduce the computation inside the model. This motivates methods that operate on the visual representation itself by selecting, pruning, caching, or reusing visual tokens.

VLA-Cache follows this direction by reusing cached representations for temporally stable patches and recomputing regions that change or remain task-relevant~\cite{vlacache}. It shows that visual computation can be reduced while largely preserving manipulation performance, and has become a reference point for later training-free VLA acceleration methods~\cite{vlapruner,efficientvla,specprune}. At the same time, VLA-Cache and VLA-Pruner show that token-reduction methods transferred directly from generic VLMs, such as FastV and SparseVLM, can degrade VLA control, since token importance for action prediction does not always align with generic visual-language saliency~\cite{fastv,sparsevlm,vlacache,vlapruner}. These findings suggest that visual efficiency in VLAs must be guided by the requirements of closed-loop action generation, rather than image or language relevance alone.

\noindent\textbf{Network Depth Reduction.}
Beyond reducing visual tokens, another line of work improves efficiency by reducing the amount of computation performed inside the policy network. ShortGPT ranks layers using Block Influence, while Gromov et al.\ remove contiguous layer groups based on representation similarity~\cite{shortgpt,gromov}. Building on this idea, EfficientVLA applies non-contiguous layer pruning to VLAs without retraining~\cite{efficientvla}, and MoLe-VLA learns an input-dependent layer router through self-distillation~\cite{molevla}. Compact VLA architectures also reduce depth at design time by using fewer language-model layers or by removing the language-model pathway from action prediction~\cite{flower,smolvla,turbovla}. Together, these methods show that some VLA backbones contain redundant depth, but the benefit of depth reduction depends strongly on the model architecture, action head, and the amount of redundancy available in the pretrained policy.

\noindent\textbf{Temporal Efficiency.}
A complementary source of efficiency comes from temporal redundancy. In robotic manipulation, consecutive observations and actions often change slowly, which motivates frame skipping and action chunking~\cite{dqn,act,diffusionpolicy,openvlaoft}. These methods reduce the number of policy calls, but they also reduce how often the policy receives updated feedback from the environment. Therefore, recent methods use state-dependent signals to decide when computation can be safely skipped or reused. FlashVLA gates action reuse based on visual and action consistency, while SpecPrune-VLA adapts computation according to action dynamics~\cite{flashvla,specprune}. Other methods reuse temporal visual information without skipping policy calls. TTF-VLA fuses current and historical visual tokens using motion and attention relevance, VLA-InfoEntropy uses entropy-based signals, and VLA-IAP relies on interaction alignment for visual-token selection~\cite{ttfvla,vlainfoentropy,vlaiap}. These approaches indicate that temporal redundancy can be exploited either at the action level or at the visual-feature level, but both require signals that are sensitive to the current interaction state.

\noindent\textbf{Static and Conditional Interventions.}
The interventions evaluated in this work can be understood through this distinction between fixed reductions and signal-driven adaptation. Visual foveation and fixed action repetition apply the same reduction throughout execution, and therefore serve as static controls. Depth pruning reduces network computation using a signal estimated once from calibration data, following the Block Influence criterion of ShortGPT~\cite{shortgpt}. In contrast, guarded reuse follows FlashVLA and SpecPrune-VLA by checking the newest observation, recent action consistency, gripper state, and translation before deciding whether to skip a policy call~\cite{flashvla,specprune}. Temporal fusion reuses visual features across frames while still querying the policy at every step, allowing temporal visual redundancy to be studied separately from policy-call skipping~\cite{ttfvla,vlainfoentropy,vlaiap}. Section~III specifies each mechanism and uses this grouping to separate static controls from conditional interventions.
