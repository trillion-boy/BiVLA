\section{Related Work}

We cover three strands: the cost of VLA inference, the training-free
interventions we re-measure, and how such interventions are evaluated.

\textbf{Inference cost in VLA policies.}
VLA policies adapt pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions,
inheriting their size and latency; surveys of the resulting efficiency
literature are now
appearing~\cite{effvlasurvey1, effvlasurvey2}. Work on reducing inference
cost falls into three families by the resource each spends:
1)~\emph{when the policy runs} --- executing a predicted chunk over several
control steps, a lineage running from frame skip in
RL~\cite{dqn} through action chunking~\cite{act, diffusionpolicy} to the
parallel decoding of OpenVLA-OFT~\cite{openvlaoft};
2)~\emph{what it is shown} --- reducing or reweighting visual tokens; and
3)~\emph{how much network each call uses} --- skipping decoder layers. We
treat these as three axes rather than as competing methods, because a claim
about efficiency is a claim about one of these resources being spent
differently.

\textbf{The interventions we re-measure.}
Layer redundancy in language models is well
established~\cite{shortgpt, gromov}, and the Block Influence criterion we
adopt is ShortGPT's~\cite{shortgpt}; EfficientVLA carries it into VLAs without
training~\cite{efficientvla}, while MoLe-VLA pursues layer skipping \emph{with}
a learned router and distillation~\cite{molevla}, which places it outside what
we test. On the visual axis, methods developed for
VLMs~\cite{fastv, sparsevlm, tome} have been reported to transfer poorly to
VLAs: VLA-Cache attributes this to VLAs emitting short action sequences and
measures FastV leaving FLOPs unchanged while latency
rises~\cite{vlacache}, and VLA-Pruner reproduces the degradation on a third
setting~\cite{vlapruner}. Foveation, our own visual intervention, is a vision
technique of the same kind, tested for the same transfer. Notably, recent
compact VLAs no longer apply these reductions at inference but build them in
--- FLOWER prunes 30--50\% of its VLM layers by design~\cite{flower}, SmolVLA
retains only the first sixteen~\cite{smolvla}, and TurboVLA removes the
language model from the action path
entirely~\cite{turbovla} --- which makes whether such a reduction transfers a
question about architecture, not only about a switch.

\textbf{How these claims are evaluated.}
Efficiency results are reported on SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} and
LIBERO~\cite{libero}, and recent work has begun to address the surrounding
infrastructure: vla-eval unifies fourteen benchmarks behind one harness and
documents previously undocumented evaluation
pitfalls~\cite{vlaeval}. What that infrastructure cannot supply is the
comparison itself. Papers that use several backbones change the benchmark at
the same time~\cite{vlacache, specprune, vlaiap}, and those with both axes
present leave the crossing cell empty~\cite{gazereg, vlapruner}; all report
mean success rates over independent runs. Consequently, whether an
intervention keeps its \emph{direction} when the backbone or the benchmark
changes cannot be read off the published tables, and neither can the
per-task disagreements those tables average over
(Table~\ref{tab:reporting}).

Unique to this paper, we measure the same interventions over a complete
backbone $\times$ benchmark grid and test each result on matched episodes
rather than on aggregate rates, so that the direction of an effect --- not
only its size --- is something the evidence can decide.
