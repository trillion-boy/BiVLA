\section{Related Work}

We cover three strands: the cost of VLA inference, the training-free
interventions we re-measure, and how such interventions are evaluated.

\textbf{Inference cost in VLA policies.}
VLA policies adapt pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions,
inheriting their size and latency; the resulting efficiency literature already
has surveys of its own~\cite{effvlasurvey1, effvlasurvey2}. It falls into three
families by the resource each spends:
1)~\emph{when the policy runs} --- executing a predicted chunk over several
control steps, from frame skip in RL~\cite{dqn} through action
chunking~\cite{act, diffusionpolicy} to OpenVLA-OFT's parallel
decoding~\cite{openvlaoft};
2)~\emph{what it is shown} --- reducing or reweighting visual tokens;
3)~\emph{how much network each call uses} --- skipping decoder layers. We
treat these as axes rather than as competing methods, because a claim about
efficiency is a claim about one of these resources being spent differently.

\textbf{The interventions we re-measure.}
Layer redundancy in language models is well
established~\cite{shortgpt, gromov}; the Block Influence criterion we adopt is
ShortGPT's~\cite{shortgpt}. EfficientVLA carries it into VLAs without
training~\cite{efficientvla}, while MoLe-VLA pursues layer skipping \emph{with}
a learned router and distillation~\cite{molevla}, which places it outside what
we test. On the visual axis, methods developed for
VLMs~\cite{fastv, sparsevlm, tome} have been reported to transfer poorly to
VLAs: VLA-Cache attributes this to VLAs emitting short action sequences and
measures FastV leaving FLOPs unchanged while latency
rises~\cite{vlacache}, and VLA-Pruner reproduces the degradation on a third
setting~\cite{vlapruner}. Our own visual intervention, foveation, comes from
the other direction: the log-polar retinotopic mapping of
Schwartz~\cite{schwartz}, adopted in robot vision to cut data while
preserving central resolution~\cite{traver}, and applied to VLAs by
gaze-conditioned policies~\cite{gazereg, lookfocusact}. We include it because
it edits the observation \emph{before} the encoder, which is what makes it
comparable across backbones: however the encoders differ, each splits the image
into a \emph{uniform} grid, so empty background receives the same budget as the
region where the gripper meets the object. Notably, recent
compact VLAs no longer apply these reductions at inference but build them in
--- FLOWER prunes 30--50\% of its VLM layers by design~\cite{flower}, SmolVLA
retains only the first sixteen~\cite{smolvla}, and TurboVLA removes the
language model from the action path
entirely~\cite{turbovla} --- which makes whether such a reduction transfers a
question about architecture, not only about a switch.

\textbf{How these claims are evaluated.}
Results are reported on SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} and
LIBERO~\cite{libero}, and recent work addresses the surrounding
infrastructure: vla-eval unifies fourteen benchmarks and documents previously
undocumented evaluation pitfalls~\cite{vlaeval}. What infrastructure cannot
supply is the comparison itself. Papers that use several backbones change the
benchmark at the same time~\cite{vlacache, specprune, vlaiap}, and those with
both axes present leave the crossing cell empty~\cite{gazereg, vlapruner}; the
tables we cite report mean success rates over independent runs, without
matched-episode outcomes. So whether an intervention keeps its \emph{direction}
when the backbone or the benchmark changes cannot be read off these tables, nor
can the per-task disagreements they average over (Table~\ref{tab:reporting}).

We do not cover quantisation, KV-cache compression or learned early exit, which
spend resources our three axes do not. Re-measuring methods one did not invent
is itself an established form~\cite{bagoftricks_cnn, bagoftricks_llm}.
Unique to this paper, we measure the same interventions over a complete
backbone $\times$ benchmark grid and test each result on matched episodes
rather than on aggregate rates, so that the direction of an effect --- not
only its size --- is something the evidence can decide.
