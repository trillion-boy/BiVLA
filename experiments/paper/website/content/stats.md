**Per-cell p-values.** Every setting's exact two-sided McNemar p-value is in the tables of 2.1. Over the 110 table cells (the best setting per trick), 22 lower success at p < 0.05 and 4 raise it: CogACT WidowX depth pruning 2 layers (+9.5, p = 0.009), OpenVLA Fractal task-aware fusion (+6.8, p = 0.012), CogACT WidowX motion-entropy fusion (+8.0, p = 0.020) and UniVLA LIBERO Goal foveation 50% (+7.0, p = 0.039). Over all 286 settings, 77 lower success and 6 raise it.

**Multiplicity.** Each table cell is the best of two or three settings, so the p-values are read as descriptive. A Benjamini-Hochberg correction at q = 0.05 over the 110 cells keeps 16 of the 22 drops and none of the 4 gains, and the expected number of chance passes is 3.1 over the 110 cells and 8.0 over the 286 settings, so the 6 gains over all settings are at chance level. Guarded reuse changes success significantly in none of its 66 settings.

**Run-to-run agreement.** On a guarded-reuse episode where the gate never fired, the original policy ran at every step, so the outcome should match the original run. It does on SpatialVLA, UniVLA, OpenVLA Fractal and OpenVLA LIBERO (0.0 to 0.7 percent of such episodes differ) and less closely on the four other WidowX pairs (CogACT 16.5, OpenVLA 13.4, CronusVLA 9.3, MiniVLA 9.1 percent; CogACT and CronusVLA on Fractal 1.8 and 4.6; the SmolVLA reuse cells 20.3). This is drift between separate runs rather than per-call randomness: CogACT's two fusion runs on WidowX, executed separately through the same code path, agree on all 200 episodes. On those pairs a change of a few points is within run-to-run drift (CogACT strict reuse, gate open on 0.3 percent of steps: +1.5, p = 0.68), which is why only the paired test is read.
