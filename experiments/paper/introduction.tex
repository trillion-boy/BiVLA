\section{Introduction}

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are becoming capable of general-purpose
robotic control, and running one is expensive. In our own runs a single call
to the policy averaged $2.81$~s of model time for UniVLA~\cite{univla} and
$0.90$~s for SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla} over 96 baseline WidowX-Bridge
episodes. OpenVLA-OFT reports autoregressive OpenVLA at $3$ to $5$~Hz, below
the $25$ to $50$~Hz it says real-time deployment needs~\cite{openvlaoft}. A
literature has grown up around fixing this without retraining. The idea is to
leave the checkpoint alone and make inference cheaper by skipping decoder
layers~\cite{shortgpt, efficientvla}, by pruning visual tokens~\cite{fastv},
or by caching what the previous step already computed~\cite{vlacache,
efficientvla}. MoLe-VLA learns which layers to skip~\cite{molevla} and is
outside what we test.

Such papers share one form of claim. \emph{Applying method $M$ to backbone $A$
on benchmark $B$ cut FLOPs by $X\%$ while success fell only $Y$ points, so $M$
is efficient.} That last step holds only if the effect belongs to the method
rather than to the configuration it was measured in, and telling those apart
takes a grid that moves the backbone and the benchmark independently. The
premise is what we test, not any individual method.

We re-measured three interventions under one protocol, on three open backbones
(OpenVLA~\cite{openvla}, SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla}, UniVLA~\cite{univla})
and two SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} suites (WidowX-Bridge, Google
Robot/Fractal). We chose these three because each spends a different resource.
Action repetition changes how often the policy runs, visual foveation (a
log-polar warp and its inverse) changes what it is shown, and network depth
pruning changes how much of the model each call uses. UniVLA releases a
Bridge-only checkpoint, so we fill five of the six cells, each with eight
conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:grid}), and the campaign comes to $7{,}198$ episodes
once its control runs, sweeps and re-runs are counted. Every condition replays
the initial states of its cell's baseline, and we test the episodes whose
outcome flipped with McNemar's exact test.

Three results follow, and each has the same shape. The factor that would get
the credit is not the factor that moved the number.

\textbf{A choice the specifications leave open moves a result by 45.9 points.}
Inside one cell we held the backbone, the benchmark, the method and the number
of layers removed (four) fixed, and varied only which layers were
\emph{eligible} for removal. Success went from $+15.6$ to $-30.4$ against the
same baseline, while measured compute stayed $10.6$ to $11.9\%$ below it. The
ranking criterion never changed. The candidate window did, and the
prescriptions on record disagree about it. The methods we re-measure rank
every layer and constrain nothing~\cite{shortgpt, efficientvla}, while a
competing one restricts removal to a contiguous block at the deep end and
reports that the final layer must be kept~\cite{gromov}. Confining removal to
the deep end is a restriction an implementation can add on its own, and ours
did. The setting that loses $30.4$ points is the one that confines it far
enough to delete the final layer. Across all five cells the contrast spans
$2.1$ to $50.4$ points, so even the sensitivity is a property of the
configuration.

\textbf{Compute saved does not predict what happens to success.} In one cell
we swept how much of the observation foveation keeps, and the best setting
discards nothing, giving $+30.2$ points at $100\%$ against $+18.8$ at $20\%$
and $+4.2$ at $10\%$. Across every foveation condition we ran, measured
compute stayed within $3.1\%$ of baseline, because neither the image size nor
the token count changes. The gain is largest where only the round trip is
left, and it is an input transformation rather than an efficiency technique.

\textbf{The same intervention reverses sign across backbones.} Under depth
pruning of four layers, OpenVLA gains $15.6$ points on the same $135$ Fractal
episodes while SpatialVLA loses $17.8$, each surviving correction on its own
and differing from the other. Average the two cells and the effect nearly
vanishes. The cell mean hides a split of its own, since \texttt{pick coke can}
comes out ahead of \texttt{move near} in ten of the fourteen intervention
conditions our two Fractal cells ran, and published SimplerEnv comparisons
print the same split without discussing it~\cite{efficientvla, vlapruner}.

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{1)~A uniform re-measurement.} We run three
training-free interventions over a $3 \times 2$ backbone-benchmark grid, five
filled cells with eight conditions each plus their controls, sweeps and
re-runs, $7{,}198$ episodes in all, and we release the per-episode records.
\textbf{2)~Evidence that a reported effect can be a property of the
configuration.} A candidate window the specifications leave open moves one
result by $45.9$ points, and one intervention reverses sign across backbones
with both directions individually significant. \textbf{3)~Evidence that
compute saved does not predict success change.} The intervention behind our
largest gain saves almost no compute and helps most when it discards nothing,
while the one whose compute saving barely moves shifts success by $45.9$
points. \textbf{4)~A measurement procedure} for this class of claim. We match
episodes, check determinism explicitly, and require every cell to run the same
conditions. Of the five training-free methods we survey~\cite{efficientvla,
vlacache, fastv, shortgpt, vlapruner}, every one sweeps its own configuration
and none publishes per-episode outcomes or tests a difference for
significance.

We propose no new efficiency method, and we do not claim that these
interventions fail in general. We measure every change against its own cell's
baseline, so no result depends on our absolute rates matching anyone else's.
Four of our five baseline cells do sit above the figures published for the
checkpoints we run, one of them by a wide margin, and the fifth is $4.2$
points below. A broken setup would push all five down. Our claim is narrower.
As results are reported now, the evidence does not separate a property of the
method from a property of the configuration it was measured in.
