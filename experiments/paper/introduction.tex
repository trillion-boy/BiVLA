\section{Introduction}

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are becoming capable of general-purpose
robotic control, and running one is expensive. In our own runs, a single call
to the policy averaged $2.81$~s of model time for UniVLA~\cite{univla} on an
L4 and $0.90$~s for SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla} on a T4. Both averages cover
96 baseline WidowX-Bridge episodes. OpenVLA-OFT reports autoregressive OpenVLA
at $3$ to $5$~Hz, below the $25$ to $50$~Hz that paper says real-time
deployment needs~\cite{openvlaoft}. A literature has grown up around fixing
this without retraining. The idea is to leave the checkpoint alone and make
inference cheaper by skipping decoder layers~\cite{shortgpt, efficientvla}, by
pruning visual tokens~\cite{fastv}, or by caching what the previous step
already computed~\cite{vlacache, efficientvla}. MoLe-VLA learns which layers
to skip~\cite{molevla} and is outside what we test.

Such papers share one form of claim. \emph{Applying method $M$ to backbone $A$
on benchmark $B$ cut FLOPs by $X\%$ while success fell only $Y$ points, so $M$
is efficient.} That last step holds only if the effect belongs to the method
rather than to the configuration it was measured in. That premise is what we
test, not any individual method. Telling the method from the configuration
takes a grid that varies the backbone and the benchmark independently.

We re-measured three interventions under one protocol, on three open backbones
(OpenVLA~\cite{openvla}, SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla}, UniVLA~\cite{univla})
and two SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} suites (WidowX-Bridge, Google
Robot/Fractal). We chose these interventions because each spends a different
resource. Action repetition changes how often the policy runs, visual
foveation (a log-polar warp and its inverse) changes what it is shown, and
network depth pruning changes how much of the model each call uses. UniVLA
releases a Bridge-only checkpoint, so we fill five of the six cells, each with
eight conditions (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). Counting the control runs and sweeps
that the results below rest on, the campaign comes to $6{,}910$ episodes.
Every condition replays the initial states of its cell's baseline, and we run
McNemar's exact test on the episodes whose outcome flipped.

Three results follow, and each has the same shape. The factor that would get
the credit is not the factor that moved the number.

\textbf{A choice the specifications leave open moves a result by 45.9 points.}
Inside one cell, we fixed the backbone, the benchmark, the method and the
number of layers removed (four), and varied only which layers were
\emph{eligible} for removal. Success went from $+15.6$ to $-30.4$ against the
same baseline, while model time per call stayed $10.6$ to $11.9\%$ below
baseline. The ranking criterion never changed. The candidate window did, and
the prescriptions on record disagree about it. The methods we re-measure rank
every layer and constrain nothing further~\cite{shortgpt, efficientvla}, while
a competing one restricts removal to a contiguous block at the deep end and
reports that the final layer must be kept~\cite{gromov}. Confining removal to
the deep end is a restriction neither method we implement asks for, and our
implementation added it anyway. The restriction stays inert while it is loose.
Widening the window from the back half of the stack to the back three quarters
selects the same four layers and returns the same $+15.6$. The setting that
loses $30.4$ points narrows it instead, far enough that only four layers
remain eligible, so the ranking has nothing left to choose and the final layer
goes with the rest. Across all five cells, the same contrast spans $2.1$ to
$50.4$ points. How much the choice matters is itself a property of the
configuration.

\textbf{What foveation discards is not what makes it help.} In one cell, we
swept how much of the observation to keep, and the best setting discards
nothing, giving $+30.2$ points at $100\%$ against $+18.8$ at $20\%$ and $+4.2$
at $10\%$. Our three backbones tokenize a fixed patch grid, so a pixel-space
edit cannot change how many tokens they process. Model time per call, which
excludes the warp itself, stayed within $3.1\%$ of baseline in every condition
we ran. The gain peaks where nothing is discarded and only the log-polar round
trip remains, so foveation without retraining is an input transformation and
not a way to spend less.

\textbf{The same intervention reverses sign across backbones.} Under depth
pruning of four layers, OpenVLA gains $15.6$ points while SpatialVLA loses
$17.8$ on the same $135$ Fractal episodes, each surviving correction on its
own and differing from the other. Averaged over the two cells, the effect
nearly vanishes. The cell mean hides a split of its own. The task \texttt{pick
coke can} comes out ahead of \texttt{move near} in ten of the fourteen
intervention conditions that our two Fractal cells ran, and the two published
SimplerEnv comparisons we cite print the same split without remarking on
it~\cite{efficientvla, vlapruner}.

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{1)~A uniform re-measurement.} We run three
training-free interventions over a $3 \times 2$ backbone-benchmark grid and
fill five of its cells, each with eight conditions. The campaign totals
$6{,}910$ episodes counting the control runs and sweeps. We release the
per-episode records. \textbf{2)~Evidence that a reported effect can be a
property of the configuration.} A candidate window the specifications leave
open moves one result by $45.9$ points, and one intervention reverses sign
across backbones with both directions individually significant.
\textbf{3)~Evidence that compute saved does not predict success change.} The
intervention behind our largest gain saves almost no compute and helps most
when it discards nothing. Depth pruning saves the same amount either way and
still swings success from a gain to a loss. \textbf{4)~A measurement
procedure} for this class of claim. We match episodes, check determinism
explicitly, and hold the condition set fixed across the cells we compare. Of
the five training-free methods we survey~\cite{efficientvla, vlacache, fastv,
shortgpt, vlapruner}, every one sweeps its own configuration and none reports
per-episode outcomes or tests a difference for significance.

We propose no new efficiency method, and we do not claim that these
interventions fail in general. We measure every change against its own cell's
baseline, so no result depends on our absolute rates matching anyone else's.
Four of our five baseline cells do sit above the figures published for the
checkpoints we run, one of them by a wide margin. The fifth is $4.2$ points
below. A broken setup would push all five down. What we do claim is narrower.
As results are reported now, the evidence does not separate a property of the
method from a property of the configuration it was measured in.
