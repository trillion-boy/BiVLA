\section{Introduction}

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are becoming capable of general-purpose
robotic control, and running one is expensive. In our own runs a single
forward pass of model time averaged $2.81$~s for UniVLA~\cite{univla} and
$0.90$~s for SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla} over 96 baseline episodes.
OpenVLA-OFT measures autoregressive OpenVLA at $3$ to $5$~Hz, below the $25$
to $50$~Hz it reports real-time deployment needs~\cite{openvlaoft}. A
literature has grown up around fixing this without retraining. The idea is to
leave the checkpoint alone and make inference cheaper by skipping decoder
layers, by pruning or caching visual tokens, or by holding one predicted
action across several control steps~\cite{efficientvla, vlacache, fastv,
shortgpt}. Layer skipping has also been pursued \emph{with}
training~\cite{molevla}, which is outside what we test.

Such papers share one form of claim. \emph{Applying method $M$ to backbone $A$
on benchmark $B$ cut FLOPs by $X\%$ while success fell only $Y$ points, so $M$
is efficient.} That last step holds only if the effect belongs to the method
rather than to the configuration it was measured in, and telling those apart
needs a grid that moves the backbone and the benchmark independently. The
premise is what we test, not any individual method.

We re-measured three interventions under one protocol, on three open backbones
(OpenVLA~\cite{openvla}, SpatialVLA~\cite{spatialvla}, UniVLA~\cite{univla})
and two SimplerEnv~\cite{simplerenv} suites (WidowX-Bridge, Google
Robot/Fractal). We chose these three because each spends a different resource.
Action repetition changes how often the policy runs, visual foveation (a
log-polar warp and its inverse) changes what it is shown, and network depth
pruning changes how much of the model each call uses. Five of the six cells
are filled, since UniVLA releases a Bridge-only checkpoint, and each carries
eight conditions, for $7{,}198$ episodes in all (Fig.~\ref{fig:grid}). Every
condition replays the initial states of its cell's baseline, and we test the
episodes whose outcome flipped with McNemar's exact test.

Three results follow, and each has the same shape. What a reported number was
credited to is not what moved it.

\textbf{A choice the specifications leave open moves a result by 45.9 points.}
Inside one cell we held the backbone, the benchmark, the method and the number
of layers removed (four) fixed, and varied only which layers were
\emph{eligible} for removal. Success went from $+15.6$ to $-30.4$ against the
same baseline, while the compute saved stayed between $-10.6$ and $-11.9\%$, a
spread no wider than repeating the same measurement produces. The ranking
criterion never changed. The candidate window did, and the two prescriptions
on record disagree about it. The methods we re-measure rank every layer and
constrain nothing~\cite{shortgpt, efficientvla}, while a competing one
restricts removal to a contiguous block at the deep end and reports that the
final layer must be kept~\cite{gromov}. Confining removal to the deep end is a
restriction an implementation can add without recording it, and ours did. The
setting that loses $30.4$ points is the one that confines it far enough to
delete the final layer. Across all five cells the contrast spans $2.1$ to
$50.4$ points, so even the sensitivity is a property of the configuration.

\textbf{Compute saved does not predict what happens to success.} Sweeping how
much of the observation foveation keeps, we find the best setting discards
nothing, giving $+30.2$ points at $100\%$ against $+18.8$ at $20\%$ and $+4.2$
at $10\%$. Measured compute moved between $-3.1\%$ and $+2.7\%$, because
neither the image size nor the token count changes. The gain tracks the
log-polar round trip, but it is an input transformation and not an efficiency
technique.

\textbf{In our grid the backbone matters more than the benchmark.} One of the
fourteen benchmark-axis comparisons survives correction, against six of the
twenty-nine along the backbone axis. On the same $135$ Fractal episodes under
the same intervention, OpenVLA gains $+15.6$ while SpatialVLA loses $-17.8$,
each surviving correction on its own and differing from the other. Averaged
over those two cells the intervention would look like nothing, and the same
cancelling happens one level further down. Within a cell, \texttt{pick coke
can} degrades less than \texttt{move near} under almost every intervention we
ran, and published SimplerEnv comparisons print that difference without
discussing it~\cite{efficientvla, vlapruner}.

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{1)~A uniform re-measurement.} Three
training-free VLA interventions over a $3 \times 2$ backbone-benchmark grid,
with five filled cells, eight conditions each, $7{,}198$ episodes, and
per-episode records released. \textbf{2)~Evidence that a reported effect can
be a property of the configuration.} A candidate window the specifications
leave open moves one result by $45.9$ points, and one intervention reverses
sign across backbones with both directions individually significant.
\textbf{3)~Evidence that compute saved does not predict success change.} The
intervention behind our largest gain saves almost no compute and helps most
when it discards nothing, while the one whose compute saving we hold fixed
moves success by $45.9$ points. \textbf{4)~A measurement procedure} for this
class of claim, comprising matched episodes, an explicit determinism check and
a grid uniformity rule. Of the five training-free methods we
survey~\cite{efficientvla, vlacache, fastv, shortgpt, vlapruner}, every one
sweeps its own configuration and none publishes per-episode outcomes or tests
a difference for significance.

We propose no new efficiency method, and we do not claim that these
interventions fail in general. Every contrast above is paired inside its own
cell, so none of them depends on our absolute rates matching anyone else's.
Four of our five baseline cells do sit above the published figures, one of
them by a wide margin, and the fifth is $4.2$ points below, which is the wrong
direction for a broken setup. Our claim is narrower. As results are reported
now, the evidence does not separate a property of the method from a property
of the configuration it was measured in.
