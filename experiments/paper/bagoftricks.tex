\section{Bag of Tricks}

\subsection{Preliminaries}

A vision-language model (VLM) maps an image and a text prompt to text through a
visual encoder and a transformer decoder over the resulting tokens. A
vision-language-action (VLA) policy adapts a pretrained VLM to control. It keeps
the encoder and decoder and attaches an action head that reads robot actions off
the decoder's output, fine-tuned on demonstrations~\cite{openvla, spatialvla,
univla}. It therefore inherits the VLM's parameter count and per-call latency,
which is the cost the interventions in this paper act on.

Concretely, write the policy as $\pi_\theta$ with a decoder of $N$ transformer
layers. An encoder turns the image and instruction into tokens, the decoder
processes them, and the action head reads out a \emph{chunk} of $c$ actions,
\begin{equation}
\pi_\theta(o_t) = (a_t^1, \dots, a_t^c),
\end{equation}
which the environment executes in order before the next query at step $t{+}c$.
The chunk length $c$ and the depth $N$ are fixed by the checkpoint and are one,
one, five and 32, 32, 26 for the three backbones we run
(Table~\ref{tab:backbones}).

Two settings govern how often the decoder runs relative to the environment. The
chunk length $c$ fixes how many environment steps one query already covers, and
an \emph{action repeat} $r$ holds each returned action for $r$ consecutive
steps. One query therefore advances the environment by $h = c\,r$ steps, a
quantity we call the \emph{horizon}. The horizon, not $r$ alone, is what a
temporal intervention changes, and because $c$ differs across backbones the same
$r$ gives a different horizon on each.

Each intervention moves one quantity and leaves the rest fixed: the horizon $h$,
the tokens the encoder is given, or the set of decoder layers a query runs. The
baseline is the released policy run unchanged, at the native chunk with $r{=}1$,
the full observation, and all $N$ layers, and every intervention perturbs a
single one of these against that baseline. We add no training.

\subsection{Methods}

\emph{Action repetition} holds every returned action for $r$ consecutive
environment steps, so a two-action chunk at $r{=}2$ executes as
$a_1a_1a_2a_2$, and we sweep $r \in \{2, 4\}$. It changes the horizon and
leaves the cost of a single query untouched.

\emph{Visual foveation} applies a log-polar warp to the observation and inverts
it before the image reaches the encoder, at keep fractions we sweep, in two
variants, log-polar and blur. The warp is a pixel-space edit that runs outside
the network.

\emph{Depth pruning} scores each decoder layer by its input-output redundancy,
the Block Influence criterion, and removes the $k$ most redundant of the $N$
layers within an eligibility window over the stack. We fix the removed count
$k$ rather than the fraction $k/N$, since $N$ is 32, 32, and 26, and sweep
$k \in \{1, 2, 4\}$.
