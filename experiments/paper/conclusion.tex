\section{Conclusion, Limitation and Future Works}

We ran six tricks, the original policy among them, on seven released VLA
policies and three environments under one matched protocol, 47,600
episodes each paired with an episode of the original policy on the same
initial state. Guarded reuse is
the safe trick. It never lowers success significantly on any of the seven
policies, but its gates rarely open, so it saves a tenth of the time at
most.
Action repeat is the fast trick and the harmful one. It is the only trick
that runs far faster than the original, and at some setting it lowers
success on six of the seven policies. Depth pruning sits between them. One removed layer buys
a small speedup on most policies and leaves success intact on three of
the seven, and each further layer adds pairs where success falls. Foveation and temporal
fusion buy no speed, foveation lowers success on three policies, and
fusion moves it only within what chance allows. No trick raises success,
and the sign of
a trick is a joint property of the rule, the policy and the environment,
so no claim transfers between combinations without a matched test on
the target. What each trick treats as redundant is
redundant on some policies and needed on others.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} Each cell is one run, so the paired
test speaks only to the tested initial states, and CogACT, CronusVLA, MiniVLA and OpenVLA do not
reproduce their WidowX rollouts under a fixed seed. LIBERO cells, with 100 episodes, have less power than SimplerEnv
cells, and guarded reuse ran fixed presets rather than thresholds tuned
per policy. Latency is read only within one backbone, environment, software stack
and GPU class, peak memory was not measured, and all runs are in
simulation. Several seeds per cell, real-robot replication of the sign
flips of Section~IV-B, and a measured integration of the fusion reuse mask
with a token cache are the next steps, and the paired records and gate
counts allow a new trick to be tested against the same
episodes.
