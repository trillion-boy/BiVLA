\subsection{Setup and Protocol}

\textbf{Backbones.} We evaluate seven released VLA policies without
retraining (Table~\ref{tab:backbones}). CogACT, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA,
CronusVLA, UniVLA and MiniVLA run on SimplerEnv, and OpenVLA, UniVLA and
SmolVLA run on LIBERO, with the checkpoint each project released for that
benchmark. Parameter counts are as reported by the original works. The
decoder depth is the stack that depth pruning acts on, the language decoder
for six backbones and the twelve layer DiT action decoder for CronusVLA.
Block Influence is defined on any residual block as one minus the cosine
similarity of the block's input and output, so it applies to the DiT blocks
unchanged, and the CronusVLA rows test that transfer rather than assume it.
Actions per call is how many environment steps one model call covers.
UniVLA executes its native chunk of five actions on SimplerEnv and ten on
LIBERO, and every other harness executes one action per call. The attention
implementation is the one each backbone ships with, resolved under the
evaluation environment with transformers 4.47.0, and SmolVLA under an
isolated 4.51.3 stack, recorded per backbone in Table~\ref{tab:backbones}.
OpenVLA requests SDPA on SimplerEnv, and its LIBERO loader defaults to eager.
%PENDING mentor: torch version and GPU column.

\textbf{Environments and episodes.} SimplerEnv WidowX has four tasks (put
carrot on plate, put eggplant in basket, put spoon on towel, stack cube) at
50 episodes each, with a step cap of 60, or 120 for the eggplant task. Google
Robot, called Fractal below, has five tasks (close drawer, open drawer, move
near, pick coke can, place apple in closed top drawer) at 50 episodes each
and a cap of 80. LIBERO has four suites (Long, Goal, Object, Spatial) of ten
tasks at 10 episodes each and caps of 520, 300, 280 and 220. Every
configuration of a backbone replays the same task instances and initial
states, with the episode seed fixed as 42 plus the episode index, so each
episode has a matched dense control. A failed episode runs to its cap. Each
checkpoint runs on the benchmark its
training data targets, Bridge for WidowX, Fractal for Google Robot and the
matching suite for LIBERO. The LIBERO checkpoints run in their training
distribution, and the WidowX and Fractal checkpoints are real-trained
policies run in simulation under SimplerEnv's visual matching protocol, so
success measures what an intervention does to an already trained policy on
its target benchmark rather than how the policy generalises.

\textbf{Configurations.} Each backbone and environment pair runs the dense
policy and thirteen settings, two or three per family. Foveation keeps a
central disc of 0.2 or 0.5 of the image area. Action repeat holds each
action for two or four steps. Depth pruning removes one, two or four
layers, chosen by Block Influence on calibration frames ahead of the test
episodes. % Calibration source recorded only for CronusVLA (seed 10000, same tasks); the disjoint claim is dropped rather than asserted. Guarded reuse runs three presets,
strict, moderate and aggressive, with whole-frame and local signature
thresholds of 0.01 and 0.03, 0.015 and 0.04, and 0.02 and 0.05, action
agreement of 0.995, 0.99 and 0.98, a translation floor of 0.01 in all
three, and a consecutive-reuse cap of one, one and two. Temporal fusion runs
motion-entropy and task-aware with a keyframe every third call and a reuse
fraction of 0.5, and conservative-adaptive with a keyframe every second
call, a reuse fraction of 0.25 and a forced keyframe when any patch moves by
more than 0.03. The motion threshold is 0.01, the entropy and attention
protection fractions 0.15 and 0.2, and the protection radius one patch. In
all, 22 backbone and environment pairs, 14 configurations each but for one
temporal fusion setting missing on OpenVLA Long, and 47,500 episodes.
%PENDING the three reruns change the CronusVLA and UniVLA WidowX counts only if a run is replaced.

\textbf{Measures.} Success is the benchmark's own criterion. Latency is
wall-clock per environment step, the episode time divided by its steps,
which has the same meaning on every harness whether a call covers one step
or a chunk. We read latency as the change from dense within a backbone.
Every harness other than CronusVLA also records its per-call time, which
Section~IV-C uses, and CronusVLA reports only the episode clock. Avg.\ Steps is the mean episode length. Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results}
and~\ref{tab:libero-results} show, for each family, the setting with the
highest success and, on a tie, the fewer average steps, with the row named
by the family. The other settings appear in the text.

\textbf{Pairing and significance.} For every cell we compare the same
episodes under the dense and the intervened policy and count the episodes
that flipped from failure to success and the reverse. A change is called
significant when an exact two-sided McNemar test on those flips gives
$p < 0.05$. The other six backbones reproduce the dense episodes up to about
one episode in a hundred, so for them the single-run limit concerns other
initial states, left to Section~V. CronusVLA does not reproduce its rollout
under the same fixed seed, so its dense and intervened runs diverge and its
cells carry a run-to-run floor of about 14 episodes in 200, reported in
Section~IV-B. Three WidowX cells rerun on a second card report success only,
and their paired test waits on the per-episode files of a dense run matched
on that card. With 110 cells this threshold admits about five chance passes, so
Section~IV-B reads gains against that count. Each table cell is the
family-best setting chosen by success, so its individual p-value is
post-selection and read as descriptive. Sixteen of the 22 significant drops
pass a false discovery rate correction at 0.05, no gain passes it, and no
gain survives a Bonferroni correction over all 285 settings that were tested.

\textbf{Hardware and implementation.} [GPU per backbone, one card model per
backbone and environment.] SmolVLA ran under two implementations, the
released lerobot stack for the dense policy, foveation, action repeat and the
depth cells it could run, and a reconstructed stack on several GPU models for
guarded reuse, temporal fusion and the depth cells that fell back to it, so
its latency is compared only within the first group. SmolVLA depth pruning removes fixed
layer indices rather than the Block Influence selection, so its depth cells
are read as unmatched to the method of Section~III. The UniVLA WidowX
task-aware run, in the two tasks that crashed, and the CronusVLA WidowX
moderate and aggressive presets were repeated on an RTX PRO 6000 after the
first runs, on an RTX 5090, crashed or ran with the strict thresholds, so
their latency is not compared either. CogACT exposes no text-to-vision
attention at the point where fusion runs, so its task-aware setting equals
motion-entropy. The task-aware fusion setting collects attention relevance,
which runs the decoder in eager attention, so its per-call time is measured
against a different backend than its own dense row.
