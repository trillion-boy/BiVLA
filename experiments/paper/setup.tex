\section{Setup and Protocol}

\textbf{The grid.} We cross three open backbones (OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, UniVLA)
with two SimplerEnv suites, WidowX-Bridge and Google Robot/Fractal. UniVLA
releases only a Bridge checkpoint, so five of the six cells are filled and
UniVLA/Fractal is left empty (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). Each filled cell runs the
same eight conditions, a baseline and seven interventions, over a fixed episode
set, 96 episodes on Bridge (four tasks at 24) and 135 on Fractal. The grid is
therefore $3 \times 96 \times 8 + 2 \times 135 \times 8 = 4{,}464$ episodes.
Beyond it, eligibility-window controls, a foveation keep sweep, extra depth
variants, determinism re-runs, and a task-level failure probe bring the
campaign to $6{,}910$. Table~\ref{tab:backbones} lists the checkpoint, decoder
depth, actions emitted per call, and GPU for each backbone.

\textbf{The three interventions, as we implement them.} We apply each to the
released checkpoint without retraining. \emph{Action repetition} holds every
predicted action for $r$ consecutive environment steps, so a two-action output
at $r{=}2$ executes as $a_1a_1a_2a_2$, and we sweep $r \in \{2, 4\}$. A backbone
emits one, one, or five actions per call (Table~\ref{tab:backbones}), so one
value of $r$ sets a different open-loop horizon on each. \emph{Visual foveation}
applies a log-polar warp to the observation and inverts it before the image
reaches the encoder, at keep fractions we sweep, in two variants, log-polar and
blur. \emph{Depth pruning} scores each decoder layer by its input-output
redundancy, the Block Influence criterion, and removes the $k$ most redundant
layers inside an eligibility window. We hold the number of removed layers fixed
rather than the fraction, since the stacks are 32, 32, and 26 layers deep, and
sweep $k \in \{1, 2, 4\}$.

\textbf{Pairing and significance.} Every condition replays the initial states
of its cell's baseline, so each episode has a matched control. We compare a
condition against that baseline with McNemar's exact two-sided test on the
discordant pairs, the episodes whose success flipped, and report the paired
change in percentage points. Across the grid this is a family of $38$ paired
tests, the $35$ drawn in Table~\ref{tab:grid} plus three depth variants that
some cells also ran. We control the family with a Bonferroni threshold of
$\alpha = 0.05/38 \approx 0.0013$, which eight of the drawn entries clear.

\textbf{Determinism.} With greedy decoding and a seeded environment, re-running
a condition reproduces it episode for episode, 85 of 85 and 24 of 24 on two
backbones. The result files do not record the GPU, so we pair only within a
cell and require a condition and its baseline to share a run environment. The
two Fractal backbones ran on the same card model (Table~\ref{tab:backbones}),
which places the cross-backbone contrast on Fractal on matched hardware.

\textbf{Baselines against the reported figures.} To check the environment
configuration, we compare each baseline against the figure published for the
same checkpoint. Four of the five sit above it, UniVLA/Bridge by $11.4$ points,
and the fifth, SpatialVLA/Bridge, is $4.2$ points below. Because every result is
a within-cell paired change, no claim depends on these rates, and a broken
setup would depress all five together rather than leave four high and one
marginally low.
