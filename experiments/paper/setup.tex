\section{Setup and Protocol}

\textbf{The grid.} We cross three open backbones (OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, UniVLA)
with two SimplerEnv suites, WidowX-Bridge and Google Robot/Fractal. UniVLA
releases only a Bridge checkpoint, so five of the six cells are filled and
UniVLA/Fractal is left empty (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). Each filled cell runs the
same eight conditions, a baseline and seven interventions, over a fixed episode
set, 96 episodes on Bridge (four tasks at 24) and 135 on Fractal. The grid is
therefore $3 \times 96 \times 8 + 2 \times 135 \times 8 = 4{,}464$ episodes.
Beyond the grid, eligibility-window controls, a foveation keep sweep, extra
depth variants, and the determinism re-runs below bring the campaign to
$6{,}910$. Table~\ref{tab:backbones} lists the checkpoint, decoder depth,
actions emitted per call, and GPU for each backbone.

\textbf{The three interventions, as we implement them.} Each spends a different
resource, and each is applied to the released checkpoint with no retraining.
\emph{Action repetition} holds every predicted action for $r$ consecutive
environment steps, so a two-action output at $r{=}2$ executes as
$a_1a_1a_2a_2$, and we sweep $r \in \{2, 4\}$. A backbone emits one, one, or
five actions per call (Table~\ref{tab:backbones}), so a single value of $r$ is
a different open-loop horizon on each, a point we return to in the results.
\emph{Visual foveation} applies a log-polar warp to the observation and inverts
it before the image reaches the encoder, at keep fractions we sweep, in two
variants, log-polar and blur. The backbones tokenize a fixed patch grid, so
this pixel-space edit leaves the token count unchanged. \emph{Depth pruning}
scores each decoder layer by its input-output redundancy, the Block Influence
criterion, and removes the $k$ most redundant layers inside an eligibility
window. We hold the number of removed layers fixed rather than the fraction,
since the stacks are 32, 32, and 26 layers deep, and sweep $k \in \{1, 2, 4\}$.

\textbf{Pairing and significance.} Every condition replays the initial states
of its cell's baseline, so each episode has a matched control. We compare a
condition against that baseline with McNemar's exact two-sided test on the
discordant pairs, the episodes whose success flipped, and report the paired
change in percentage points. Across the grid this is a family of $38$ paired
tests, the $35$ drawn in Table~\ref{tab:grid} plus three depth variants that
some cells also ran. We control the family with a Bonferroni threshold of
$\alpha = 0.05/38 \approx 0.0013$, which eight of the drawn entries clear.

\textbf{Determinism.} We ran repeats to check that a paired change is a
property of the intervention and not of the run. Re-running a condition in the
same environment reproduces every episode, 85 of 85 and 24 of 24 on two
backbones, and a third condition run in July and again in August agrees on all
96. Moving the same condition to a different GPU model flips 11 of 96 episodes,
a 3.1-point gap. We therefore require a condition and its baseline to share one
environment, and rebuilt the UniVLA/Bridge column on its single-card runs so
that no delta is taken across that boundary.

\textbf{Baselines against the reported figures.} Because every result is a
within-cell paired change, no claim depends on our absolute rates matching
anyone else's. We report the comparison anyway, as the direct answer to a
broken-setup objection. Four of the five baseline cells sit above the figures
published for the same checkpoints, UniVLA/Bridge by $11.4$ points, and the
fifth, SpatialVLA/Bridge, is $4.2$ points below. A broken setup would depress
all five together rather than leave four high and one marginally low.
