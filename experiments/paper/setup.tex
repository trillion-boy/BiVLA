\subsection{Setup and Protocol}

\textbf{Backbones.} We evaluate seven released VLA policies without
retraining. CogACT (7.6B, 32 Llama decoder layers), OpenVLA (7B, 32
Llama-2 layers), SpatialVLA (4B, 26 Gemma2 layers), CronusVLA (0.5B, a
12-layer DiT action decoder), UniVLA (8.5B, 32 Emu3 layers) and MiniVLA
(1B, 24 Qwen2.5 layers) run on SimplerEnv, and OpenVLA, UniVLA and SmolVLA
(0.45B, 32 SmolLM2 layers) run on LIBERO, each on the checkpoint released
for that benchmark, named in the code release. Depth pruning acts on the
decoder stack named for each. Every backbone emits one action per call
except UniVLA, whose chunk covers five steps on WidowX and ten on LIBERO.
CogACT, CronusVLA, MiniVLA and SpatialVLA run under transformers 4.47.0
and SmolVLA on an isolated 4.51.3 stack, and OpenVLA requests SDPA on
SimplerEnv but defaults to eager on LIBERO.

\textbf{Environments and episodes.} SimplerEnv WidowX has four tasks at 50
episodes each, with a step cap of 60, or 120 for the eggplant task. Google
Robot, called Fractal below, has five tasks at 50 episodes each and a cap of
80. LIBERO has four suites (Long, Goal, Object, Spatial) of ten tasks at 10
episodes each and caps of 520, 300, 280 and 220. Every
configuration of a backbone replays the same task instances and initial
states, with the episode seed fixed as 42 plus the episode index, so each
episode has a matched dense control. A failed episode runs to its cap. The
LIBERO checkpoints run in their training distribution and the WidowX and
Fractal checkpoints under SimplerEnv's visual matching protocol, so success
measures what a trick does to a trained policy on its target
benchmark rather than how the policy generalizes.

\textbf{Configurations.} Each backbone and environment pair runs the dense
policy and thirteen settings, two or three per trick. Foveation keeps a
central disc of 0.2 or 0.5 of the image area, action repeat holds each
action for two or four steps, and depth pruning removes one, two or four
layers chosen by Block Influence on calibration frames disjoint from the
test episodes.
% Matches the Method section wording (disjoint). Recorded calibration source (CronusVLA: seed 10000, same tasks) uses a seed distinct from the test seed 42+index, so its calibration episodes are disjoint from the test episodes.
Guarded reuse runs the strict, moderate and aggressive presets and
temporal fusion the motion-entropy, task-aware and conservative-adaptive
settings of Section~III. In all, 22 backbone and environment pairs, 14
configurations each, and 47,600 episodes.
% 2026-09-13: OpenVLA Long conservative-adaptive arrived (45/100), so all 22 pairs have 14 configurations. The three reruns replaced runs at the same n, so no count changed.

\textbf{Measures.} Success is the benchmark's own criterion. Latency is
wall-clock per environment step, the episode time divided by its steps,
which has the same meaning on every harness whether a call covers one step
or a chunk. We read latency only as the change from dense within one backbone, one
environment, one software stack and one GPU class, and cells that break
this rule report success alone.
Every harness other than CronusVLA also records its per-call time, which
Sections~IV-B and~IV-C use, and CronusVLA reports only the episode clock.
Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results} and~\ref{tab:libero-results} show,
for each trick, the setting with the highest success and, on a tie, the
fewer average steps, and the figures show every setting.

\textbf{Pairing and significance.} Every cell compares the same episodes
under the dense and the intervened policy, and a change is called
significant when an exact two-sided McNemar test on the episodes that
flipped between failure and success gives $p < 0.05$. The test pools the
tasks of a cell. The figures show point estimates, and this test decides
significance. Each table cell is the best of two or three settings,
so its p-value is post-selection and read as descriptive, and
Section~IV-B reads gains over all 286 settings, where about 14
chance passes are expected at this threshold, about seven per direction.
Sixteen of the 22 significant table drops pass a false discovery rate
correction at 0.05 over the 110 table cells, and no gain does. CronusVLA
does not reproduce its rollout under a fixed seed. Its strict reuse cell
fired on 5 of 9,947 steps and still differs from dense on 14 of 200 WidowX
episodes, which we take as its run-to-run floor, so its WidowX cells within
that floor are read as no change, and no floor was measured on Fractal.
The other six backbones reproduce the dense outcome on all but about one
episode in a hundred.

\textbf{Hardware and implementation.} Runs were scheduled on a shared GPU
cluster with VRAM class matched within a backbone, and the card is
recorded where known, CronusVLA and UniVLA WidowX on an RTX 5090.
SmolVLA's guarded reuse and temporal fusion ran under a reconstructed
stack on other cards, so their latency is not read, and three runs, UniVLA
WidowX task-aware and the CronusVLA WidowX moderate and aggressive presets,
were repeated on an RTX PRO 6000 after failing on the RTX 5090. Three
table cells whose gates never fired differ from their dense row by 0.4 to
1.4 percent in latency, which we take as the latency floor. CogACT exposes
no text-to-vision attention at the point where fusion runs, so its
task-aware setting equals motion-entropy. Collecting attention relevance
for the task-aware setting forces an SDPA decoder into eager attention. On
OpenVLA, whose LIBERO dense row already runs eager, task-aware adds the
same eight percent per call as on its SDPA rows, so that cost is the
relevance collection, and on MiniVLA and UniVLA the two are not separated.
