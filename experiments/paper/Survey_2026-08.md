## 1. The headline: the claim survives, and it got stronger

I flagged SpecPrune-VLA and VLA-IAP as probably filling the crossing cell.
**Both PDFs say otherwise, and for the same reason.**

### SpecPrune-VLA (arXiv 2509.05614) — two backbones, two benchmarks, never together

Table 1 splits cleanly:

| benchmark | backbone used |
|---|---|
| LIBERO (Spatial/Object/Goal/Long) | **OpenVLA-OFT** only |
| SimplerEnv (the same four Bridge tasks we run) | **DB-OFT** only |

So changing the benchmark changes the backbone. That is **exactly the design
flaw §2.6 already names for VLA-Cache** — with both moving at once, a sign
comparison is not available even in principle. Training-free confirmed (four
uses, no "retrain"); no occurrence of *paired* or *McNemar* anywhere.

**And it hands us a quotable sentence for test 3 of the axis filter:**

> *"VLA-Cache and EfficientVLA is originally designed for VLM-based VLA
> models, therefore, **we only implement them on OpenVLA-OFT**."*

A published paper stating that two efficiency methods could not be applied
uniformly across its own backbones. That is our uniformity argument, made by
someone else.

### VLA-IAP (arXiv 2603.22991) — four backbones, three benchmarks, no shared pair

The Table 1 caption settles it:

> *"We evaluate **DreamVLA**, **π0 (LIBERO)** and **π0.5 (VLABench)** across
> multiple benchmarks…"*

| backbone | benchmarks |
|---|---|
| DreamVLA | LIBERO + CALVIN |
| π0 | LIBERO only |
| π0.5 | VLABench only |
| OpenVLA-OFT | LIBERO only (Table 2) |

Both axes exist, but **no two backbones share a second benchmark**, so there is
no complete 2 × 2. Ours has one: OpenVLA and SpatialVLA, both on Bridge and
Fractal. Training-free confirmed (it is in the title).

### The pattern is now the finding

Three independent papers — VLA-Cache, SpecPrune-VLA, VLA-IAP — change the
backbone when they change the benchmark. Gaze-Reg and VLA-Pruner have both
axes and leave the crossing cell empty. **Five papers, five different ways of
not asking the question.**

That is much better than "we are not aware of anyone doing this." It is a
structural observation with five instances, and it explains *why* the question
goes unasked: the crossing cell is the expensive one, and nothing in current
practice rewards filling it.

**Novelty claim, final form:**

> Prior work evaluates efficiency methods across backbones or across
> benchmarks, but not the same intervention across both, and reports mean
> success rates rather than matched-episode outcomes. We fill a complete
> 2 × 2 and test the sign directly.

Both halves now verified from source, and the second half — pairing — is
unchallenged: none of the papers read contains *paired*, *McNemar*, or a
significance test of any kind.

## 2. Two adjacent papers that change the framing of contribution 4

Our fourth contribution is "a measurement procedure for this class of claim."
Two 2026 papers occupy nearby ground and must be cited and distinguished.

| paper | what it does | how ours differs |
|---|---|---|
| **vla-eval** (MAUM.AI / AI2 / SNU) — *read from the PDF* | An **infrastructure** paper: WebSocket+msgpack protocol, Docker isolation, 14 benchmarks × 6 model servers, 47× parallel speedup (2,000 LIBERO episodes in ~18 min), a 657-result leaderboard. It reproduces published scores across six codebases and *"document[s] previously undocumented pitfalls"* | **Complementary, not competing — and the word counts prove it.** In the whole paper: *paired* 0, *significance* 0, *statistic* 0, *confidence* 0, *error bar* 0. It answers "how do I run everything"; we answer "what can the numbers support." Cite it, and say that ours is the statistical half |
| **StarVLA** — *"A Lego-like Codebase for Vision-Language-Action Model Developing"*, StarVLA Community & Von Neumann Institute, HKUST — **read from the PDF 2026-08-22** | Abstract: *"VLA methods remain fragmented across incompatible architectures, codebases, and evaluation protocols, hindering principled comparison and reproducibility."* §1: *"At the evaluation level, results are reported on disjoint subsets of benchmarks with inconsistent protocols, making fair comparison infeasible,"* creating a *"Tower of Babel"* for VLA research | **that is our problem statement, already in print.** Cite it as agreeing with us rather than let a reviewer find it first |

> ⚠️ **Correction, 2026-08-22.** This row previously quoted StarVLA as saying
> *"most existing VLA methods are evaluated on limited environments with
> substantially different preprocessing pipelines, policy interfaces, and
> evaluation protocols."* **That sentence does not appear in the PDF.** It came
> from the search snippet this section was built from, and it was set in
> quotation marks as if it were the paper's own words. The three quotes now in
> the row are verbatim and were located in the PDF (abstract and §1).
>
> The claim itself survives unchanged — StarVLA does say this, in its own
> words. What failed was the quoting, which is exactly the risk that made us
> refuse to cite it from a snippet in the first place. That refusal was right.

**Neither is a threat — both are support.** vla-eval's own abstract complains
of *"underspecified evaluation protocols"* and *"reverse-engineering
undocumented preprocessing"*; StarVLA says the same in different words. Two
2026 papers independently stating our motivation is better than stating it
alone. What has to change is only the tone: our Introduction currently reads
as if we are the first to notice.

## 4. The training-free landscape, as far as abstracts show

Everything below claims training-free in its title or abstract. **None of these
is verified from a PDF** — and the MoLe-VLA lesson is that abstracts are not
evidence. They are listed so the sweep is on record, not so they can be cited.

| paper | arXiv | axis | claims training-free |
|---|---|---|---|
| SpecPrune-VLA | 2509.05614 | token pruning + self-speculative | yes ⚠ |
| Token Expand-Merge (TEAM-VLA) | 2512.09927 | token compression | yes ⚠ |
| VLA-InfoEntropy | 2604.05323 | entropy-gated dynamic inference | yes ⚠ |
| VLA-IAP | 2603.22991 | token pruning | yes ⚠ |
| DepthCache | 2603.10469 | depth-guided token merging | yes ⚠ |
| SAFE-Pruner | 2605.29662 | future-aware token pruning | yes ⚠ |
| SP-VLA | 2506.12723 | model scheduling + token pruning | yes ⚠ — OpenVLA-7B, LIBERO only |
| Environment-Aware Adaptive Pruning | 2602.00780 | token pruning | yes ⚠ |
| Action-aware Dynamic Pruning | 2509.22093 | token pruning | yes ⚠ |
| TTF-VLA | 2508.19257 | temporal token fusion | yes ⚠ |

**What this list means for us.** The token-pruning axis is *crowded* — ten-plus
papers in eighteen months. That is an argument for **not** adding it as a
fourth axis to compete on, and for using FastV instead as the *object* of
measurement: an established method that two independent groups already report
differently on VLM versus VLA. Our value is not another pruner; it is asking
whether any of them holds its sign.
