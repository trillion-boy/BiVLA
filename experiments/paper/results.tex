\subsection{Results and Discussion}

Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results} and~\ref{tab:libero-results} give the
best setting of each trick on every backbone and environment, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser} and Figs.~\ref{fig:tradeoff-widowx}
to~\ref{fig:consistency} show every configuration. We read them through three questions, one for each part of the
control loop that a trick touches.
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\labelwidth}{2.9em}\setlength{\labelsep}{0.4em}%
  \setlength{\leftmargin}{3.3em}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}%
  \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}}
\item[\textbf{RQ-1:}] Does changing \emph{what} the policy sees or
\emph{how much} it computes ever raise success, and which tricks leave
success intact?
\item[\textbf{RQ-2:}] Which tricks make a policy faster, and what does
each unit of speed cost?
\item[\textbf{RQ-3:}] Does a trick that helps one policy help another?
\end{list}

\textbf{RQ-1.} Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser} shows every configuration on the
six WidowX backbones, and no bar rises more than 10 points above the
dotted line of the original, while the bars that fall 10 points or more
below it belong to action repeat, depth pruning and, on OpenVLA and
UniVLA, foveation. Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results}
and~\ref{tab:libero-results} say the same for every environment, with
few and small green success cells against many and large red ones. Of the 110 best-setting cells, 22
lower success significantly and four raise it, and the four gains, on
CogACT WidowX, OpenVLA Fractal and UniVLA Goal, are of the size and
number that chance allows over this many comparisons, while most of the
drops exceed 10 points. Two tricks never lower success significantly in
their best cell on any of the seven backbones, guarded reuse and temporal
fusion, and guarded reuse does so in none of its 66 settings. The other
three each lower it on several backbones, foveation on three, action
repeat and depth pruning on four each at their mildest setting.
\textbf{[A-1] No trick raises success. Guarded reuse and temporal fusion
leave it intact on every backbone, and foveation, action repeat and depth
pruning each cost success on about half of them.}

\textbf{RQ-2.} Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff-widowx} places every setting by its
speedup and its success change, and the favorable corner, faster and no
worse, is nearly empty on every backbone. Action repeat is the only trick
far to the right, at 1.4 to 1.9 times the original's speed per step with two steps
and up to 3.6 with four, and it is also the trick that falls furthest,
with significant drops on 8 of 22 backbone and environment pairs at two
steps and 20 at four (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation-depth}). Depth pruning moves
a little to the right and, on some backbones, far down. One removed
layer is faster than the original on 14 of the 22 pairs and costs success
significantly on 8, and four layers are faster on 21 and cost success on
14. Guarded reuse saves time only where its gates open, a tenth at most on
OpenVLA LIBERO and under 5 percent elsewhere, and reports the original policy
under its own name where they stay shut. Foveation makes every backbone but UniVLA
slower, and task-aware fusion adds about a tenth to every call on
OpenVLA, MiniVLA and UniVLA, since the blur and the attention collection
add time without removing any computation. \textbf{[A-2] Action repeat
is the fastest trick and the most harmful, depth pruning buys a small
speedup on most backbones at a success cost that the backbone sets, and
the tricks that touch what the policy sees buy no speed at all.}

\textbf{RQ-3.} The sign of a trick changes with the backbone and the
environment. In Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff-widowx} depth pruning sits above
the original on CogACT and far below it on MiniVLA and SpatialVLA, on the same
tasks and episodes. Foveation at the same keep ratio raises UniVLA and
lowers SmolVLA on the same LIBERO suite. Action repeat at two steps
lowers success on all six WidowX backbones and leaves the four Fractal
backbones where they were (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation-depth}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:consistency} counts this over the 13 backbone and
environment combinations. No setting is above the original on more than six,
and the settings that most often leave it unchanged are the ones whose
gates rarely fire. \textbf{[A-3] A trick does not transfer.
Its sign is a joint property of the rule, the backbone and the
environment, so a gain on one combination says nothing about the next
without a matched test there.}

\subsection{Analysis}

\textbf{Episode length follows success.} Every failed episode ran to its
step cap, so Avg.\ Steps in the tables tracks the Success column and is
not an independent measure of efficiency.

\textbf{What a repeated call saves.} Per-step latency under action repeat
lands above the halving that the call count predicts, because the
simulator step is paid on every environment step and only the model
computation scales with the calls. The same accounting bounds guarded
reuse at its reuse share, about a tenth of the call time at most, and
fusion at zero.

\textbf{What the per-episode record adds.} Two settings can report the
same success rate on different episodes. On UniVLA WidowX, motion-entropy
and task-aware fusion both lose 11 of 200 episodes net, and only the first
loss is significant, because the two settings flip different episodes.
A setting can also report the original policy under its own name. The UniVLA
guarded reuse cells fired on at most five steps and match the original on every
episode but one, and conservative-adaptive fusion selected no reusable
patch on SpatialVLA and UniVLA. We therefore report how often each gate
fired and recommend the same for any gated trick. Two caveats remain.
CronusVLA's cells carry the run-to-run floor of Section~IV-A, and its
three fusion cells are one run whose settings were not recorded.
