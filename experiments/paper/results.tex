\subsection{Results and Discussion}

Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results} and~\ref{tab:libero-results} report the
Success, Latency and Avg. Steps columns for the dense policy and for the
best setting of each family on every backbone and environment, with the
change from dense in parentheses. Latency is wall-clock per environment
step. Each cell is the best of two or three settings by the rule of
Section~IV-A. Of the 110
intervention cells (22 backbone and environment pairs across five families),
26 change success significantly, 22 of them downward. With 110 comparisons, about five would
pass the threshold by chance alone, and the four gains, depth pruning and
motion-entropy fusion on CogACT WidowX ($+9.5$ and $+8.0$ points),
task-aware fusion on OpenVLA Fractal ($+6.8$) and foveation on UniVLA Goal
($+7.0$), are of that size and number. The drops are not. Twenty of the 22
remove 10 points or more, and the largest, action repeat on UniVLA, removes
58 to 75. We therefore read the drops as effects and the gains as
observations to replicate. Four
cells whose intervention never fired reproduce the dense outcome on every
episode, and two others with no fired step differ from dense on one and two
episodes of 100 and 200, so run-to-run variation is about one episode in a
hundred and the one-seed limitation concerns other initial states rather
than measurement noise.

\textbf{Cells that are not what their row says.} Some cells carry a row
label their run did not earn, and we name them once here. The UniVLA WidowX
and LIBERO Spatial fusion cells are the conservative-adaptive setting, which
was ineffective there and selected no reusable patch, so both cells equal
the dense policy. The five UniVLA guarded reuse cells fired on 0 to 5 steps
of 6,000 to 65,600 and match the dense outcome on every episode but one. The SpatialVLA WidowX reuse cell fired on 38 steps of 9,025 and differs
from dense on three episodes. The CronusVLA WidowX reuse cell fired on 5
steps of 9,947 and still differs from dense on 14 of 200 episodes, so that
harness does not reproduce its rollout under the fixed seed. The CronusVLA fusion
cells are one run whose fusion settings were not recorded, so the three
fusion settings are identical.
The UniVLA WidowX task-aware run and the CronusVLA WidowX moderate and
aggressive presets were repeated on another GPU after the first runs
crashed or ran with the strict thresholds, so their success counts are used
and their latency is not compared.
SmolVLA's guarded reuse, temporal fusion and higher depth budgets ran under
a second implementation on other GPUs, so we read no latency change from
those cells.
The OpenVLA Long fusion cell is the better of two settings, since the third
did not run there.

\textbf{Action repeat.} Holding each action for two steps halves the model
calls per environment step and lowers latency per step to between 0.52 and
0.72 of dense. On WidowX it lowers success on every backbone, by 15.5 to
75.0 points on OpenVLA, CronusVLA, CogACT and UniVLA (all $p < 0.001$) and by
4.0 and 6.0 points on SpatialVLA and MiniVLA, neither significant. On Fractal
the four backbones with a Fractal checkpoint
change by $-1.2$ to $+4.4$ points, none significant, so the same setting
fails on WidowX and passes on Fractal for CogACT, OpenVLA and CronusVLA. On
LIBERO it removes 58 to 75 points from UniVLA on every suite ($p < 0.001$)
and changes OpenVLA and SmolVLA by $-3$ to $-12$ points, none significant.
UniVLA emits a chunk of five actions per call on WidowX and ten on LIBERO,
so one call covers ten or twenty steps without fresh feedback. Holding each
action for four steps lowers success significantly in 20 of the 22 cells.

\textbf{Foveation.} Latency per step rises in every table cell, by 3 to 16
percent on every backbone except UniVLA, whose blur is amortized over a
chunk. Success falls significantly on OpenVLA in both SimplerEnv
environments ($-13.5$ and $-11.2$) and on LIBERO Long ($-12.0$), on SmolVLA
on three of four suites ($-13.0$ to $-21.0$), and on UniVLA WidowX ($-8.5$).
It rises significantly once, on UniVLA Goal ($+7.0$). CogACT, CronusVLA,
SpatialVLA and MiniVLA stay within 5 points of dense in every environment. A
keep ratio of 0.2 rather than 0.5 removes 23 to 28 points from OpenVLA on
Fractal, LIBERO Long and LIBERO Spatial, where 0.5 removes 12 or fewer, and
on WidowX it ties or leads 0.5 for CogACT, CronusVLA, SpatialVLA and UniVLA
by up to 6.0 points.

\textbf{Depth pruning.} Removing decoder layers lowers latency per call on
every backbone except SmolVLA, monotonically in the budget, and lowers
latency per step by 1 to 6 percent in the table cells of the other six
backbones. On CogACT WidowX a budget of two layers raises success from 50.0
to 59.5 percent ($p = 0.009$), and on CogACT Fractal no budget
changes it by more than 1.6 points. On the same WidowX task instances a
budget of one removes 17.5 points from MiniVLA ($p < 0.001$) and 6.5 from
SpatialVLA ($p = 0.024$), and budgets of two and four remove all 36.0
points from MiniVLA and 17.0 and 31.0 from SpatialVLA. OpenVLA changes by
$+4.0$ and $+5.2$ points on the two SimplerEnv environments, neither
significant, loses 16.0 on LIBERO Spatial ($p = 0.011$), and loses 22 to 34
points on every suite at a budget of four. CronusVLA, whose pruned stack is
its twelve layer action decoder, changes by $+2.0$ and $+0.8$ at budgets of
two and one and loses 4.4 and 5.5 points at a budget of four. UniVLA
changes by $-5.5$ on WidowX ($p = 0.052$) and by $+1$ to $+3$ on LIBERO.
SmolVLA loses 14 to 33 points at a budget of one on every suite. The budget
is a different fraction of the 32 layer CogACT, 26 layer SpatialVLA or 24
layer MiniVLA stack and selects different layers on each, so the budget
alone does not predict the sign.

\textbf{Guarded reuse.} No guarded reuse cell changes success significantly,
and every table cell is within 5 points of dense. The per-step record of the
gates bounds the effect. Reuse fires on one step in ten for OpenVLA on
LIBERO under the aggressive setting, on 2 to 6 percent of steps for OpenVLA
elsewhere and for MiniVLA, and on under 5 percent for the other backbones.
On UniVLA it fires on fewer than one step in a thousand, and on SpatialVLA
WidowX and CronusVLA WidowX on 2 to 38 steps of 9,000 to 9,950. Latency per
step changes by under 4 percent on SimplerEnv and falls by 2 to 10 percent
for OpenVLA on LIBERO, in proportion to the steps skipped. Loosening the
gates from strict to aggressive raises
the reuse share by 1.3 to 4.5 times where reuse fires on more than 1 percent
of steps, and every setting stays
within 7 points of dense, none significant.

\textbf{Temporal fusion.} Motion-entropy and conservative-adaptive change
latency per call by 3.5 percent or less, and the attention collection of
task-aware adds 7 to 9 percent per call on OpenVLA, 14 on MiniVLA and 10 to
16 on UniVLA LIBERO. Success rises significantly on CogACT WidowX under
motion-entropy ($+8.0$, $p = 0.020$) and on OpenVLA Fractal under task-aware
($+6.8$, $p = 0.012$), and by 7.0 points at $p > 0.1$ on OpenVLA WidowX and
LIBERO Spatial. The remaining cells lie between $-7.0$ and $+6.0$, none
significant. CogACT exposes no text-to-vision attention at the point where
the selector runs, so its task-aware setting reduces to motion-entropy and
CogACT has two fusion settings. On SpatialVLA and UniVLA the
conservative-adaptive setting was ineffective and selected no reusable
patch, so it equals the dense policy on UniVLA and differs from dense on two
episodes on SpatialVLA WidowX. The SpatialVLA cells and the UniVLA Long,
Goal and Object cells therefore come from the other two settings. On UniVLA
WidowX both of those, motion-entropy and task-aware, lose 5.5 points (164 of
200 against 175), which is significant for motion-entropy ($p = 0.035$) but
not for task-aware ($p = 0.071$), the same net loss on different episodes.
On OpenVLA WidowX the two settings differ by 9.5 points, $+7.0$ under
task-aware and $-2.5$ under motion-entropy, neither significantly different
from dense. Fusion is the
candidate designed for accuracy rather than time, and it changes success
significantly in two of the 20 cells in which it ran, both gains, and both
within the chance count above.

\textbf{Across families.} Three patterns hold across the tables. First, the
sign of an intervention is not a property of the rule. Depth pruning adds
9.5 points to CogACT and removes 17.5 from MiniVLA on the same WidowX task
instances, and foveation adds 7.0 to UniVLA and removes 13.0 from SmolVLA on
the same Goal suite, the two gains within the chance count. Second, the sign is not a
property of the backbone either. Action repeat removes 38.0 points from
CogACT on WidowX and 1.2 on Fractal, and a keep ratio of 0.5 removes 14.5
points from UniVLA on WidowX ($p < 0.001$) and adds 7.0 on Goal. Third,
latency and success do not move together. Foveation is slower on every
backbone and worse on three, depth pruning is faster on six backbones and
worse on four, and action repeat is fastest and worst. The one cell that is
both faster and better, depth pruning on CogACT WidowX, reads 0.0 on
Fractal. Taken together, the sign of a training-free intervention is a joint
property of the rule, the backbone and the environment rather than of any
one of them, and an efficiency or accuracy claim made on one combination
does not transfer to another without a matched test on the target.

\subsection{Analysis}

\textbf{Episode length follows success.} Every failed episode ran to its
step cap, 60 or 120 steps on WidowX, 80 on Fractal and 220
to 520 on LIBERO, so the mean episode length is a success-weighted average
of the length of the successful episodes and the cap. The Avg. Steps columns
therefore track the Success columns and are not an independent measure of
efficiency. CronusVLA on Fractal is the exception, where 121 successful
episodes across its 14 runs also end at the cap.

\textbf{What a repeated call saves.} Latency per step under action repeat is
0.52 to 0.72 of dense at two steps and 0.28 to 0.58 at four, against the
0.50 and 0.25 that the call count alone predicts. The simulator step is paid
on every environment step, and on the SimplerEnv harnesses other than UniVLA
the elapsed time per call grows by about 120 to 230 milliseconds under four
steps,
since the call covers the steps it holds. Only the model computation scales
with the number of calls. The same accounting bounds guarded reuse, whose
saving is the reuse share times the call time and peaks at about 10
percent.

\textbf{Where the drops sit.} The per-episode record separates two ways an
intervention loses success in this study. Action repeat loses it across
every task of a backbone at once. Under two steps on WidowX every UniVLA
task falls, 42, 49, 44 and 40 successes of 50 to 12, 3, 6 and 4. Depth
pruning and foveation lose it in particular tasks. MiniVLA's depth loss at a
budget of one is stack cube, 36 to 8 successes of 50 while the other three
tasks move by at most 6, SpatialVLA's at a budget of four is put eggplant in
basket, 50 to 8, with carrot on plate unchanged, and OpenVLA's foveation
loss on WidowX is put eggplant in basket, 37 to 12, with spoon on towel
rising from 19 to 24.

\textbf{Where the signal admits it.} The two run-time candidates act only
where their signal fires. Guarded reuse needs a stable image, two agreeing
inferred actions and a commanded translation above the floor on the same
step, and finds all three
on at most 11 percent of steps. Conservative-adaptive
fusion, with its smaller reuse fraction, shorter keyframe interval and
forced keyframes, finds no reusable patch on SpatialVLA and UniVLA, and
task-aware fusion finds no text-to-vision attention on CogACT. Their signals
bound the candidates before their effect does, and a backbone on which the
signal never fires reports the dense policy under the intervention's name.
A protocol that pairs episodes and keeps the per-step record of the gates
and selectors exposes this, and one that reports aggregate rates alone does
not. A latency figure reported without the per-step gate record cannot show
whether the gate fired at all, so we report how often each reuse and fusion
gate fired and recommend the same for any gated intervention.
