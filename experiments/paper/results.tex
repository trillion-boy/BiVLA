\subsection{Results and Discussion}

Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results} and~\ref{tab:libero-results} report
Success, Latency and Avg.\ Steps for the dense policy and for the
family-best setting on every backbone and environment, with the change from
dense in parentheses, and Figs.~\ref{fig:success-widowx}
to~\ref{fig:ablation-repeat} show every tested setting on WidowX and the
depth and repeat sweeps on every pair. We read them through
four questions. RQ1 asks whether any intervention reliably improves success
or efficiency. RQ2 asks whether the sign of an intervention transfers across
backbones and environments. RQ3 asks whether computation, runtime and
success move together. RQ4 asks what the per-episode record shows that
aggregate rates hide.

\textbf{RQ1. Does any intervention reliably improve success or
efficiency?} Of the 110 table cells, 26 change success significantly, 22
downward and four upward. With 110 comparisons about five would pass the
threshold by chance, and the four gains, depth pruning and motion-entropy
fusion on CogACT WidowX ($+9.5$ and $+8.0$), task-aware fusion on OpenVLA
Fractal ($+6.8$) and foveation on UniVLA Goal ($+7.0$), lie between $+6.8$
and $+9.5$ points on three backbone and environment pairs. The drops do
not. Twenty of the 22 remove 10 points or more, and action repeat on UniVLA
removes 58 to 75. Over all 285 tested settings the count is 76 significant
drops against six significant gains, and the six gains sit on the same
three pairs. No setting raises success on more than six of the 13 backbone
and environment combinations, and every setting lowers it on at least
three (Fig.~\ref{fig:consistency}). Guarded reuse changes success
significantly in none of its 66 settings, and its largest change is 7.0
points. On the efficiency side only action repeat and depth pruning lower
latency per step, by 28 to 48 percent at two steps and by 1 to 6 percent
in the depth pruning table cells of six backbones, while SmolVLA's depth
cells stay within 1.5 percent of dense, and both pay for it in success on
some backbone (RQ3). \textbf{No intervention is a reliable gain. Significant
changes are drops by more than twelve to one, the gains are of the size
and number that chance allows, and the one family that is never harmful is
harmless because its gates rarely fire (RQ4).}

\textbf{RQ2. Does the sign of an intervention transfer across backbones
and environments?} Fig.~\ref{fig:success-widowx} shows the same 13
settings on the six WidowX backbones, and no setting other than action
repeat keeps one sign down the column. Action repeat is negative on all
six WidowX backbones, yet on Fractal the two-step setting moves the four
backbones by $-1.2$ to $+4.4$ points, none significant, so even the one
setting with a fixed sign on WidowX loses it in the next environment.
Depth pruning at a budget of two adds 9.5 points to CogACT
($p = 0.009$) and removes 36.0 from MiniVLA and 17.0 from SpatialVLA (both
$p < 0.001$) on the same WidowX task instances. Foveation at a keep ratio
of 0.5 adds 7.0 to UniVLA ($p = 0.039$) and removes 13.0 from SmolVLA
($p = 0.015$) on the same Goal suite (Table~\ref{tab:libero-results}). The
environment changes the outcome for a fixed backbone and rule as well. The
same keep ratio removes 14.5 points from UniVLA on WidowX ($p < 0.001$)
and adds 7.0 on Goal, and action repeat at two steps costs CogACT 38.0
points on WidowX and 1.2 on Fractal. The dose does not restore transfer.
Raising the depth budget from one to two to four lifts the number of
significant drops from 8 to 9 to 14 of 22, while CogACT WidowX moves by
$+7.0$, $+9.5$ and $+2.5$ and MiniVLA WidowX by $-17.5$, $-36.0$ and
$-36.0$, so the knob that collapses one backbone peaks and recedes on
another (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation-depth}, and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation-repeat}
for action repeat).
\textbf{The sign is a joint property of the rule, the backbone and the
environment. Each of the three sign flips above is significant on both
sides, and no setting is a significant gain on more than one backbone and
environment pair, CogACT's task-aware cell being its motion-entropy run
under another name (RQ4).} Across the 22 pairs a keep ratio of 0.5 matches
or beats 0.2 in 18, so the milder setting is the safer default, with the
exceptions on WidowX for CronusVLA, SpatialVLA and UniVLA and on Object for
UniVLA.

\textbf{RQ3. Do computation, runtime and success move together?}
Foveation changes no model computation, is slower in every table cell by 3
to 16 percent on every backbone except UniVLA, whose blur is amortized over
a chunk, and lowers success significantly in seven cells. Temporal fusion
changes which tokens enter the decoder and not how many, moves latency per
call by 3.5 percent or less under motion-entropy and conservative-adaptive,
and adds about 8 percent per call on OpenVLA and 14 to 17 on MiniVLA and
UniVLA under task-aware. Action repeat is the fastest family, 0.52 to 0.72
of dense latency per step at two steps, and the worst, with significant
drops in 8 of 22 cells at two steps and 20 of 22 at four. Depth pruning
lowers latency per call at budgets of two and four on every backbone that
records it, and at a budget of one on all of those but CogACT Fractal and
SmolVLA, where it stays
within one percent of dense, while its success response at one budget spans
$+9.5$ to $-36.0$. The right panels of Figs.~\ref{fig:ablation-depth}
and~\ref{fig:ablation-repeat} show the two extremes. On UniVLA LIBERO a
large per-step gain coexists with a 58 to 75 point loss, and the only
significant gain that also lowers latency, depth pruning at a budget of two
on CogACT WidowX, reads 0.0 on Fractal at its family-best budget of four.
\textbf{Lower computation implies neither lower latency nor preserved
success. Foveation and fusion change success without changing
computation, and action repeat and depth pruning change computation with a
success cost that the backbone sets.}

\textbf{RQ4. What does the per-episode record show that aggregate rates
hide?} Three things. First, the same aggregate change can carry different
evidence. On UniVLA WidowX motion-entropy and task-aware fusion both lose
5.5 points, 164 of 200 against 175, and the loss is significant for
motion-entropy ($p = 0.035$) and not for task-aware ($p = 0.071$), because
the two settings flip different episodes. Second, an intervention can
report the dense policy under its own name. The five UniVLA guarded reuse
cells fired on 0 to 5 steps of 6,000 to 65,600 and match dense on every
episode but one, the conservative-adaptive fusion cells on UniVLA WidowX
and Spatial selected no reusable patch and equal dense, and CogACT's
task-aware setting equals motion-entropy because the harness exposes no
text-to-vision attention where the selector runs. Reuse fires on about
one step in ten at most anywhere, so the null result of that family is a statement
about its gates rather than about reuse. Third, the record separates two
kinds of loss. Action repeat loses across every task at once, 42, 49, 44
and 40 successes of 50 on UniVLA WidowX falling to 12, 3, 6 and 4, while
depth pruning and foveation lose in particular tasks. MiniVLA's depth loss
at a budget of one is stack cube, 36 to 8 successes of 50, with the other
three tasks moving by at most 6, and OpenVLA's foveation loss on WidowX is
put eggplant in basket, 37 to 12, with spoon on towel rising from 19 to
24. \textbf{A paired record with per-step gate counts tells a real effect
from a silent gate and a task-specific failure from a global one.
Aggregate success rates do neither.} Two cells are read with a caveat. The
CronusVLA WidowX reuse cell fired on 5 steps of 9,947 and still differs
from dense on 14 of 200 episodes, the floor Section~IV-A states, and its
three fusion cells are one run whose settings were not recorded. The
conservative-adaptive fusion cell on OpenVLA Long, the last to arrive,
loses 11.0 points (45 of 100) and is reported without a paired test.

Taken together, the sign of a training-free intervention is a joint
property of the rule, the backbone and the environment rather than of any
one of them, and an efficiency or accuracy claim made on one combination
does not transfer to another without a matched test on the target.

\subsection{Analysis}

\textbf{Episode length follows success.} Every failed episode ran to its
step cap, 60 or 120 steps on WidowX, 80 on Fractal and 220 to 520 on
LIBERO, so the mean episode length is a success-weighted average of the
length of the successful episodes and the cap. The Avg.\ Steps columns
therefore track the Success columns and are not an independent measure of
efficiency. CronusVLA on Fractal is the exception, where a share of its
successful episodes also runs to the cap.

\textbf{What a repeated call saves.} Latency per step under action repeat
is 0.52 to 0.72 of dense at two steps and 0.28 to 0.58 at four, against
the 0.50 and 0.25 that the call count alone predicts. The simulator step is
paid on every environment step, and on the SimplerEnv harnesses other than
UniVLA the elapsed time per call grows by about 120 to 230 milliseconds
under four steps, since the call covers the steps it holds. Only the model
computation scales with the number of calls. The same accounting bounds
guarded reuse, whose saving is the reuse share times the call time and
peaks at about 10 percent, and it bounds fusion at zero, since fusion
changes no call and no token count.

\textbf{Where the signal admits it.} The two run-time candidates act only
where their signal fires. Guarded reuse needs a stable image, two agreeing
inferred actions and a commanded translation above the floor on the same
step, and finds all three on one step in ten for OpenVLA on LIBERO under
the aggressive setting, on 2 to 6 percent of steps for OpenVLA elsewhere
and for MiniVLA, on under 5 percent for the other backbones, and on fewer
than one step in a thousand for UniVLA. Loosening the gates from strict to
aggressive raises the reuse share to about one step in ten at most, and
every setting stays within 7 points of dense. Conservative-adaptive fusion, with its smaller reuse fraction,
shorter keyframe interval and forced keyframes, finds no reusable patch on
SpatialVLA and UniVLA. Their signals bound the candidates before their
effect does, and a backbone on which the signal never fires reports the
dense policy under the intervention's name. A latency figure reported
without the per-step gate record cannot show whether the gate fired at
all, so we report how often each reuse and fusion gate fired and recommend
the same for any gated intervention.
