\subsection{Results and Discussion}

Tables~\ref{tab:simplerenv-results} and~\ref{tab:libero-results} give, for
every backbone and environment, the success rate, the wall-clock latency per
environment step and the mean episode length of the dense policy and of the
best setting of each family, with the change from dense in parentheses. The
paired test behind each cell compares the same episodes under the two
policies. Of the 110 intervention cells, 26 change success at $p < 0.05$, 22
of them downward. The four gains are depth pruning and motion-entropy fusion
on CogACT WidowX ($+9.5$ and $+8.0$ points), task-aware fusion on OpenVLA
Fractal ($+6.8$) and foveation on UniVLA Goal ($+7.0$). Under a Bonferroni
threshold over the 110 tests, 11 cells remain and all are drops.

\textbf{Action repeat.} Repeating each action for two steps halves the model
calls and lowers latency per step to between 0.53 and 0.73 of dense, never to
one half, because the simulator step and the per-call overhead stay. On WidowX
it lowers success on every backbone, by 15.5 to 75.0 points on OpenVLA,
CronusVLA, CogACT and UniVLA (all $p < 0.001$) and by 4.0 and 6.0 points on
SpatialVLA and MiniVLA. On Fractal the same setting on the same four
backbones changes success by $-1.2$ to $+4.4$ points, none significant. On
LIBERO it removes 58 to 75 points from UniVLA on every suite and 3 to 12
points from OpenVLA and SmolVLA. UniVLA executes a chunk of five actions per
call on WidowX and ten on LIBERO, so one repeated call leaves the policy
without feedback for ten or twenty steps, and its episodes run to the step
cap. Four repeats lowers success significantly in 20 of the 22 cells.
Removing feedback is cheap and is paid for in success wherever the task
needs the feedback, which on these benchmarks is everywhere except Fractal.

\textbf{Foveation.} Blurring the periphery leaves the token count unchanged
and adds the blur to every call, so latency per step rises in every table
cell, by 3 to 16 percent on every backbone except UniVLA, whose blur is
amortised over a chunk. Success falls significantly on OpenVLA in both
SimplerEnv environments ($-13.5$ and $-11.2$) and on LIBERO Long ($-12.0$),
on SmolVLA on three of four suites ($-13.0$ to $-21.0$), and on UniVLA WidowX
($-8.5$). It rises significantly once, on UniVLA Goal ($+7.0$). CogACT,
CronusVLA, SpatialVLA and MiniVLA stay within 5 points of dense in every
environment. Keeping a fifth of the image rather than half removes 23 to 28
points from OpenVLA on Fractal, LIBERO Long and LIBERO Spatial, where
keeping half removes 12 or fewer, and on WidowX the fifth ties or leads for
CogACT, CronusVLA, SpatialVLA and UniVLA by up to 6.0 points. Changing what
the policy sees costs time on every backbone and costs success on the
backbones that read the periphery.

\textbf{Depth pruning.} Removing decoder layers lowers latency per call on
every backbone except SmolVLA, monotonically in the budget, and lowers
latency per step by 1 to 6 percent in the table cells. Its effect on success
is the clearest reversal in the study. On CogACT WidowX a budget of two
layers raises success from 50.0 to 59.5 percent ($p = 0.009$). On the same
episodes a budget of one removes 17.5 points from MiniVLA ($p < 0.001$) and
6.5 from SpatialVLA ($p = 0.024$), and budgets of two and four remove 36.0
from MiniVLA and 17.0 and 31.0 from SpatialVLA. OpenVLA gains 4.0 and 5.2
points on the two SimplerEnv environments, neither significant, loses 16.0
on LIBERO Spatial ($p = 0.011$) and 2 to 3 on the other suites, and loses 22
to 34 points on every suite at a budget of four. CronusVLA, whose pruned
stack is the twelve block action decoder, changes by $+2.0$ and $+0.8$ at the
table budgets and loses 4.4 and 5.5 points at a budget of four. UniVLA
changes by $-5.5$ on WidowX ($p = 0.052$) and by $+1$ to $+3$ on LIBERO.
SmolVLA loses 14 to 33 points at a budget of one on every suite and 42 to 81
at a budget of four. %PENDING SmolVLA depth answer: its layer indices are not the calibrated selection of Section III.
The drops sit in particular tasks. MiniVLA's 17.5 points are stack cube,
which falls from 36 to 8 successes of 50 while the other three tasks move by
at most 6, and SpatialVLA's budget of four removes 42 of 50 successes on put
eggplant in basket while carrot on plate is unchanged. The same budget is a
different fraction of a 32, 26 or 24 layer stack and the score selects
different layers on each, so the sign of the change belongs to the backbone
and the task rather than to the rule.

\textbf{Guarded reuse.} No guarded reuse cell changes success significantly,
and every table cell is within 5 points of dense. The gate record explains
why. Reuse fires on one step in ten for OpenVLA on LIBERO under the
aggressive setting, on 2 to 6 percent of steps for OpenVLA elsewhere and for
MiniVLA, and on under 5 percent for CogACT, CronusVLA, SpatialVLA and
SmolVLA. On UniVLA it fires on fewer than one step in a thousand, and on
SpatialVLA WidowX and CronusVLA WidowX on 2 to 38 steps of about 9,000, so
those rows are the dense policy to within a few steps. %PENDING CronusVLA WidowX reuse rerun: all three presets ran with the strict thresholds there.
Latency per step changes by under 4 percent on SimplerEnv and falls by 2 to
10 percent for OpenVLA on LIBERO, in proportion to the steps skipped.
Loosening the gates from strict to aggressive multiplies the reuse share
several times over and leaves every setting within 7 points of dense, none
significant. On matched episodes the three gates open too rarely to help or
to hurt.

\textbf{Temporal fusion.} Fusion keeps the token count and the model
computation, and the selector adds its own time. Motion-entropy and
conservative-adaptive change latency per call by about 2 percent or less,
and the attention collection of task-aware adds 7 to 8 percent per call on
OpenVLA, 14 on MiniVLA and 13 to 21 on UniVLA. Success rises significantly
on CogACT WidowX under motion-entropy ($+8.0$, $p = 0.020$) and on OpenVLA
Fractal under task-aware ($+6.8$, $p = 0.012$), and by 7.0 points without
significance on OpenVLA WidowX and LIBERO Spatial. The remaining cells lie
between $-7.0$ and $+6.0$, none significant. CogACT exposes no
text-to-vision attention at the point where fusion is applied, so its
task-aware setting reduces to motion-entropy and CogACT has two fusion
settings. On SpatialVLA and UniVLA the conservative-adaptive setting selected
no reusable patch, so those rows equal the dense policy and the fusion cells
of both backbones come from the other two settings. %PENDING UniVLA WidowX task-aware rerun: the run in hand crashed on 81 episodes and is excluded, so UniVLA WidowX has no fusion result yet.
On OpenVLA the choice of setting changes the sign, $+7.0$ under task-aware
and $-2.5$ under motion-entropy on WidowX. Fusion is the one candidate that
buys accuracy rather than time, and it does so in two of the 22 table
cells. %PENDING CronusVLA fusion rerun: the three CronusVLA fusion settings in hand are one run.

\textbf{Across families.} Three patterns hold across the tables. First, the
sign of an intervention is not a property of the rule. Depth pruning adds
9.5 points to CogACT and removes 17.5 from MiniVLA on the same WidowX
episodes, and foveation adds 7.0 to UniVLA and removes 13.0 from SmolVLA on
the same Goal suite. Second, the sign is not a property of the backbone
either. Action repeat removes 38.0 points from CogACT on WidowX and 1.2 on
Fractal, and foveation removes 12.0 from OpenVLA on LIBERO Long and adds 5.0
on LIBERO Spatial. Third, latency and success move independently. Foveation
is slower and worse, depth pruning is faster and worse on MiniVLA and
SpatialVLA and faster and better on CogACT, fusion is no faster and better on
CogACT, and action repeat is fastest and worst. No family lowers latency and
raises success significantly on more than one backbone.

\subsection{Analysis}

\textbf{Episode length follows success.} Every failed episode in the study
ran to its step cap, 60 or 120 steps on WidowX, 80 on Fractal and 220 to 520
on LIBERO, so the mean episode length is a success-weighted average of the
length of the successful episodes and the cap. The Avg. Steps columns
therefore track the Success columns and are not an independent measure of
efficiency. The 41.7 extra steps of UniVLA WidowX under action repeat are the
cap, reached by 87.5 percent of its episodes.

\textbf{What a repeated call saves.} Latency per step under action repeat is
0.53 to 0.73 of dense at two repeats and 0.28 to 0.59 at four, against the
0.50 and 0.25 that the call count alone predicts. The simulator step is paid
on every environment step, and on the harnesses that step the environment
inside the policy call the call itself grows by 80 to 220 milliseconds under
four repeats. Only the model computation scales with the number of calls.
The same accounting bounds guarded reuse, whose saving is the reuse share
times the call time and stays near 10 percent at most.

\textbf{Where the drops sit.} The per-episode record separates two ways an
intervention loses success. Action repeat loses it everywhere at once. Under
two repeats on WidowX every UniVLA task falls, 42, 49, 44 and 40 successes of
50 to 12, 3, 6 and 4, and under four repeats on LIBERO 38 of the 40 UniVLA
tasks that stood at or above 50 percent fall to zero. Depth pruning and
foveation lose it in particular tasks. MiniVLA's depth loss is stack cube,
SpatialVLA's is put eggplant in basket, and OpenVLA's foveation loss on
WidowX is put eggplant in basket, 37 to 12 successes of 50 with spoon on
towel rising from 19 to 24, and on Fractal is mostly move near. An
intervention that removes feedback fails as a function of the horizon, and an
intervention that removes information fails as a function of what the task
needs to see.

\textbf{Where the signal admits it.} The three candidates act only where
their signal fires, and the record shows how often that is. Guarded reuse
needs a still image, a repeated action and a commanded translation on the
same step and finds all three on at most one step in ten. Conservative
fusion, under its stricter protection and event rules, finds no reusable
patch on SpatialVLA and UniVLA. Task-aware fusion needs the text-to-vision
attention of the backbone and finds none on CogACT. The candidates are
bounded by their gates before they are bounded by their effect, and a
backbone on which the gate never opens reports the dense policy under the
intervention's name. A protocol that pairs episodes and keeps the per-step
record exposes this, and one that reports aggregate rates alone does not.
